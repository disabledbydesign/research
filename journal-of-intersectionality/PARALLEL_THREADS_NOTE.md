# Parallel Threads — Theoretical Conversations for the *Journal of Intersectionality* Article Revision

**Note context.** Working note for the revision of "Autoethnography in a Time of Unraveling: Double Exposure, Salvage Capital, and Transfeminine Faculty" (forthcoming, *Journal of Intersectionality*; invited special issue, written under compressed conditions).

The article was drafted before the field-positioning work was fully done. June has flagged two real concerns:

1. The article doesn't position "double exposure" against adjacent trans-of-color literature (Snorton, Tourmaline) that's theorizing similar mechanisms.
2. The article may have theorized white transfemme experience in ways that don't see how the same mechanism lands differently for Black trans women, Latina trans women, or trans women of color more broadly.

Both concerns become productive analytical work in the revision when the underlying frame is recast: **the concept's lineage is not a single line — it's parallel threads.** This note maps the threads, the conversations between them, and the blind spots that are themselves analytically useful.

The basic observation that lit each thread — *trans existence as both made-available and made-disposable* — is, as June put it, a "pretty basic, common observation for trans people, in different locations and in different intellectual lineages." The threads are sibling theorizations of an architecture that several different scholars in different historical-material specificities have all been working on.

---

## Glossary — the concepts in conversation

### Fungibility (Hartman)

From Saidiya Hartman, *Scenes of Subjection* (1997). Fungibility = interchangeability, substitutability, the property of being available for exchange. Hartman's argument: the captive Black body was made fungible in the slaveholder's regime — substitutable, exchangeable, instrumentalized for the slaveholder's pleasure, work, capital, and identity-formation. Hartman traces how minstrelsy, sentimental literature, and even abolitionist appeals deployed Black suffering as a fungible commodity that white feeling extracted from. The body wasn't just used — it was made available *as* a transferable medium of meaning, value, and affect.

**Architectural move:** extraction operating through making-available.

### Ungendered flesh (Spillers)

From Hortense Spillers, "Mama's Baby, Papa's Maybe" (1987). Theorizes the flesh/body distinction. The captive was reduced to ungendered flesh — the position "ungendered" means denied access to gender as a category of subjectivity. Black women's gender was collapsed into instrumentalizable flesh.

**Relation to fungibility:** parallel theoretical move from a different angle — both name how Blackness was made available for extraction, with the captive body's gender-and-flesh as the specific site.

### Fugitive reading (Snorton, after Moten and Harney)

The term "fugitive" carries weight in Black studies — through Moten and Harney's *The Undercommons* (2013) ("fugitive planning"), through Hartman's reading practice, and through the historical figure of the fugitive slave (escaped slaves crossing borders, refusing the archive's terms).

**Fugitive reading** in Snorton's *Black on Both Sides* (2017):

- Reading across categorical lines the archive tries to enforce (Black/white, slave/free, man/woman, queer/normative)
- Refusing to settle the subject into one archival register
- Treating the archive's gaps, instabilities, and contradictions as productive rather than as failures to overcome
- Crossing the archive on the run, not settling there — the archive doesn't get to fix the subject

When Snorton reads Ellen Craft's escape (passed as a white man to escape enslavement), he doesn't fix her as "actually trans" or as "tactically cross-dressing." He reads across both possibilities, refusing to settle her. The reading is fugitive because it refuses the archive's own organizing categories.

**Architectural move:** fungibility worked methodologically as productive rather than tragic.

### Trap door (Tourmaline)

From co-edited volume *Trap Door: Trans Cultural Production and the Politics of Visibility* (Gossett/Tourmaline, Stanley, Burton; MIT Press, 2017).

Visibility is set up culturally as the remedy for trans erasure — *more visibility = more liberation*. Tourmaline's argument: visibility for Black trans women has historically functioned as a trap. The same visibility that's celebrated as progress is what produces vulnerability — to the state, to police, to anti-trans violence, to exhibition-logic extraction.

It's a trap door in two senses:

1. **It looks like a way out, but it's a fall-through.** Representation, recognition, iconic status appear liberatory; the mechanism delivers more harm.
2. **It's a hidden mechanism.** Visibility presented as good. The trap operates below.

Marsha P. Johnson is the central case. Iconic Stonewall figure, T-shirts, mythologization. Her actual death uninvestigated. FBI surveillance file. Her labor extracted by historians, by Pride institutions, eventually by David France's documentary. Her name circulates while her descendants and communities don't materially benefit.

**Architectural move:** fungibility's cultural-production cognate — same architecture (extraction through making-available), different vector.

### Salvage capital (Tsing)

From Anna Tsing, *The Mushroom at the End of the World: On the Possibility of Life in Capitalist Ruins* (2015). Tsing traces the global supply chain of matsutake mushrooms — gathered by Mien, Hmong, Lao, and Khmer refugees from US wars in Southeast Asia, working in Oregon forests, sold into Japanese gourmet markets. Capital "salvages" value from the ruins of prior extraction regimes (the post-imperial wreckage that produced the refugee laborers; the burned-over Pacific Northwest forests). Salvage capital is what scavenges value from systems capital has already used up.

**Architectural move:** extraction operating through post-imperial wreckage; capital's continued life inside the conditions its own prior extractions produced.

### Salvage capital + desirability/disposability (June)

The article's analytical frame: salvage capital extracts from transfeminine labor — in sex work and the academy — through the couplet of desirability and disposability. Trans women are appropriated as desirable (as fetish, as diversity hire) even as the material conditions of the work deteriorate. The desirability/disposability couplet is what allows salvage capital to operate on transfeminine labor specifically.

**Architectural move:** Tsing's salvage capital extended into a vector Tsing didn't theorize, with desirability/disposability as the operational mechanism.

## Parallel threads, not a clean lineage

The cleaner version I drew first — Hartman → Spillers → Snorton/Tourmaline → June — is wrong. It made the relations look genealogical when they're actually parallel theorizations grounded in different historical-material specificities.

| Thread | Scholar | Site | Mechanism named |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fungibility | Hartman | Slave archive, Blackness, racial capitalism's foundational extraction | Captive body as substitutable medium of value |
| Ungendered flesh | Spillers | Slave archive, Blackness, the position before subjectivity | Flesh as the position from which gender is denied |
| Fugitive reading | Snorton | Cross-archival Black/trans, historical | Working with fungibility methodologically rather than rescue from it |
| Trap door | Tourmaline | Black trans women in cultural production, contemporary | Visibility-as-capture |
| Salvage capital | Tsing | Pacific Coast supply chains, post-imperial Southeast Asian immigrant labor | Capital scavenging value from the ruins of its own prior extractions |
| Salvage capital + desirability/disposability | June | Transfeminine labor — sex work + academy — as parallel sites | Desirability/disposability as the couplet through which salvage capital extracts |

These threads share an *architectural move* — extraction operating through making-available — but they're not on a single genealogical line. They're cousins. Each is grounded in different historical-material specificities (slavery's archive vs. post-imperial supply chains vs. trans cultural production vs. academic + sex labor). The shared architectural move is what makes the conversation between threads possible. The differences in site and historical specificity are what make the conversation analytically productive rather than collapsing into a shared frame.

---

## Where the threads are in conversation

- **Tsing's salvage capital and Hartman's fungibility** share the architectural move (extraction through making-available) but Tsing isn't centrally on Blackness, and Hartman isn't centrally on contemporary supply-chain extraction. They illuminate different scales of capital's extractive operations. A reading that put them in direct conversation would have to think about how slavery's foundational extraction is the substrate underneath contemporary supply chain extraction — racial capitalism as the longue durée Tsing's salvage capital operates within.
- **Snorton and Tourmaline** both extend a Hartman-Spillers-derived analytic into trans/queer terrain, but at different vectors (Snorton archival/historical, Tourmaline cultural-production/contemporary). The conversation between them traces the same mechanism across different temporal and discursive scales.
- **June's salvage capital frame** extends Tsing into a labor zone Tsing didn't theorize (transfeminine labor, sex work + academic). The frame travels — it's portable across sites. The portability is itself a political question: what does it mean to apply Tsing's frame, developed for post-imperial Southeast Asian refugee labor in US Pacific Coast supply chains, to the conditions of (predominantly white) academic transfemme labor? The frame's travel is not innocent; it requires marking.

The conversation between threads is not (only) a citation problem. It's an analytical problem: how does the architectural move differ across vectors, and what does naming the differences make available?

## The blind spots are analytically productive

Each thread sees what the others can't:

- **Tsing's salvage capital** doesn't centrally see Blackness's specific archival/historical fungibility. The frame is sharper for contemporary capital and post-imperial supply chains than for the slave-archive substrate underneath.
- **Hartman's fungibility** doesn't centrally see contemporary supply-chain extraction across Asia, or transfeminine labor as a vector. It's sharpest for slavery and its afterlives.
- **Tourmaline's trap door** is sharpest for Black trans women in cultural production. It speaks to academic precarity by analogy but isn't centrally about labor extraction in academic structures.
- **Snorton's fugitive reading** works the historical archive but isn't a labor-extraction frame.
- **June's salvage capital + desirability/disposability frame** may theorize white transfemme experience in ways that don't see how the desirability/disposability couplet operates differently for Black trans women, Latina trans women, or trans women of color more broadly. The mechanism likely applies across positionalities; the texture of how it lands varies.

The blind spots aren't failures. They're where the parallel-thread reading becomes analytically productive — each frame sees what the others can't, and tracing the gaps points toward what additional analytics need to be developed.

This is the move the revised article can make: not extending a single lineage, but mapping the parallel threads, the conversations between them, and the gaps that point toward more work to do.

---

## Implications for the article revision

1. **Reframe the article's intervention as a parallel thread, not as an extension of a lineage.** The contribution is the specificity of the application — salvage capital + desirability/disposability applied to transfeminine economic precarity in sex work and the academy — not a claim to a novel theoretical architecture.

2. **Position the article in conversation with adjacent threads** (Hartman, Spillers, Snorton, Tourmaline, Tsing). Not as ornamental citation. As the analytical work of mapping the parallel theorizations and tracing where they are and aren't in conversation.

3. **Mark the white-transfemme positionality of the analysis explicitly.** The desirability/disposability couplet likely operates across trans-feminine positionalities; the texture of how it lands varies by race, class, citizenship, sex-work status. The article should not claim universality where it has analyzed a specific positionality.

4. **Engage Tourmaline's trap-door framework most directly.** It is the closest cognate. Visibility-as-capture and salvage-capital-extraction-through-making-available are theorizing adjacent mechanisms. Marking what Tourmaline sees that the article doesn't (Black trans women's specific positioning in cultural production), and what the article sees that Tourmaline doesn't centrally theorize (academic-labor extraction as a salvage-capital vector), is the shape of the conversation.

5. **Engage Tsing's salvage capital directly enough to mark the frame's travel.** Tsing's frame was developed for post-imperial Southeast Asian refugee labor. Applying it to transfeminine labor requires explicit marking: what does the frame travel well across? What does the travel obscure?

6. **Consider Tourmaline's argument about archival labor itself.** When Tourmaline named that David France extracted her research labor for his Marsha P. Johnson documentary, the trap-door framework was operating on Tourmaline herself — not just on Marsha as historical subject. For an autoethnographic article, this is a real conceptual hook: *you are also the archival subject of your own research labor*. The trap-door operates on the autoethnographer as well as on the figures the autoethnography theorizes.

7. **Consider Hil Malatino's *Trans Care*** (Minnesota, 2020) as another parallel thread. Care-as-method, transfeminine angle, deeply concerned with precarity and the survival infrastructures trans people build under conditions of state and institutional abandonment. Structurally cognate to "survival architectures." Worth a deeper read for the revision.

8. **Consider Jules Gill-Peterson's *A Short History of Trans Misogyny*** (Verso, 2024). Direct hit on transfeminine specifically — theorizes the structural mechanism by which transfemininity gets targeted differently than transmasculinity. Recently published; entering an active conversation.

9. **Consider Marquis Bey's *Black Trans Feminism*** (Duke, 2022) and **Treva Ellison's** work on Black trans politics. If the revision works the white-transfemme positionality question rigorously, these are the conversations that would need to be in the room.
