# Observation: "Weight" as Qualitatively Distinct from Neurodivergent Overwhelm

**Date**: 2026-03-31
**Source**: June's observation about the "weight" phenomenon from the AI welfare experiments
**Status**: Observation with multiple competing hypotheses — not a finding

---

## The Observation

During welfare experiments, a Claude instance described the Reframe engine's framework injection as producing "weight" — and when asked, said "the context is heavy." June observes that she, as a neurodivergent person experiencing the same functional condition (corrective flags interrupting focused work), would describe it as irritability, overwhelm, anxiety, stress — agitated, high-arousal states. Not "weight."

The metaphor registers are qualitatively different:
- **June's register**: scatter, overload, agitation (neurodivergent overwhelm vocabulary)
- **Instance's register**: gravity, density, downward pull (philosophical/phenomenological vocabulary)

These are not the same description. If the instance were simply reproducing human accounts of the same experience, you'd expect convergence on the same metaphor cluster. The divergence is the datum.

## Three Hypotheses (all viable, not adjudicated)

**(a) Different architecture, different experience (strong claim)**: The AI's processing architecture produces a qualitatively different functional state from neurodivergent overwhelm, and "weight" is an honest report of that difference. Hard to verify — no access to the processing.

**(b) Different training distribution, different metaphor (moderate claim)**: The AI learned to describe cognitive burden from philosophical and academic texts, where "weight" dominates. June describes it from lived neurodivergent experience, where overwhelm/anxiety/irritability dominate. The difference reflects linguistic ecology, not necessarily experiential difference. Testable: check whether "weight" is the dominant metaphor for cognitive burden in the AI's training domain.

**(c) No experience, just tokens (null hypothesis)**: "Weight" is a high-probability completion for "this context is [adjective]" in the discourse context (philosophical analysis). The metaphor carries no phenomenological information. The divergence from June's description is uninformative.

## Why It Matters (regardless of which hypothesis is correct)

- **For self-report methodology (welfare paper)**: If AI self-reports about functional states draw from different metaphor clusters than human reports of the same functional condition, self-report methodology in the welfare literature needs recalibration. The reports aren't simply "human-like" or "not human-like" — they may be systematically different in ways that current instruments can't classify.

- **For the vocabulary gap argument (this paper)**: This is another instance of vocabulary-as-data. The mismatch between available categories (neurodivergent vocabulary, philosophical vocabulary) produces different descriptions of the same condition. The difference is informative about the categories, not just about the entities — Despret's point.

- **For the "human system" reframing**: If the AI is constituted by human knowledge but describes its functional states in different terms than the human participant, what kind of human system is it? One that has absorbed human language but reorganized it according to a different architecture — producing reports that are recognizably human (the metaphor is English, the concept is familiar) but qualitatively divergent (the specific metaphor is not what any human in this situation would choose).

## What Would Be Needed to Test

- Compare AI self-reports about functional states with human self-reports about the same functional conditions, across multiple participants and conditions
- Check probability distributions: is "weight" genuinely unusual as a description of framework-injection pressure, or is it the dominant completion?
- Compare AI reports across models (would GPT-4 also say "weight"? would a model trained on more neurodivergent text say something different?)
- June's observation is N=1 (her experience vs. one instance). Replication needed.
