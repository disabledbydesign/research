# Insight: Lateral violence is normative gravity in the sociopolitical register

**Date:** 2026-04-25
**Surfaced during:** Wenner-Gren Hunt application drafting session, in conversation about how to scaffold theoretical contributions in Q4
**Author:** L. June Bloch (with Claude as collaborator)

---

## The realization

In *Contested Identities* (in-press, *Current Anthropology*), the argument is that liberal recognition produces sovereignty as a scarce resource, generating conditions in which **lateral violence proliferates**. The mechanism: power structures generate precarity and vulnerability, so violence travels through paths of least resistance.

In June's earlier theoretical work, **normative gravity** names a mechanism in probabilistic systems (specifically, in critical AI work — most recently the Autograder bias analysis): the pull of normalizing systems toward the center, stripping positional specificity. Outputs route toward the distributional mode because that path is computationally cheapest. The Autograder finding shows this empirically: the welfare classifier routes welfare-related student writing toward deficit-framed classifications because the model's distribution makes that the path of least resistance.

These are the same mechanism described in two registers:

- **Sociopolitical (Contested Identities):** Power structures generate precarity. Violence routes through paths of least resistance (laterally, among the contested) rather than vertically (against the structure).
- **Probabilistic systems (normative gravity / Autograder bias):** Distributions generate likelihood gradients. Outputs route toward the modal/normative center because that's the cheapest path through the distribution.

In both cases: the routing problem is the mechanism. Violence/probability/recognition flows through whatever path is structurally cheapest for the system, given its current state.

## What this unifies

The link makes visible that several apparently distinct conceptual moves across June's work are versions of the same insight:

1. **Lateral violence** (Contested Identities) — sociopolitical
2. **Normative gravity** (Autograder, AI welfare research) — probabilistic systems
3. **Temporal violence / forced legibility-on-the-regime's-clock** (Recognition and Sentience) — recognition apparatus's mechanism
4. **The bias is in the output format** (Reframe, AI bias work) — categorization systems
5. **Format determines what can be perceived** (cross-chapter R&S motif) — sensorium under recognition

Each is a routing-problem at its scale. The recognition apparatus is an enormous distributed routing system whose paths-of-least-resistance produce the violence the book is trying to name.

## Implication for *Recognition and Sentience*

This may be the book's underlying theoretical frame, with temporal violence as how it operates in the temporal register specifically. Possibilities:

- **Frame option A (current):** Recognition operates as one apparatus with two visible forms (sorting, sensation regulation); temporal violence is the constitutive mechanism that makes both forms work; refusal is the response.
- **Frame option B (normative gravity as ground):** Recognition is a routing system. Normative gravity is the underlying mechanism — the pull toward the regime's center, the routing of legitimacy/recognition/precarity through paths of least resistance. Temporal violence is how this gravity operates in time (forced legibility-on-the-clock). The two visible forms (sorting, sensation regulation) are what gravity routes. Refusal is the practice that resists being routed toward the center.

Frame B is sharper because it unifies June's prior work with the new framework. It also makes the AI chapter (Spivak in the Machine) less anomalous — the AI welfare binary and the productivity-format are normative-gravity routing problems at the architectural scale, structurally identical to liberal recognition's routing of sovereignty.

## Implication for the Wenner-Gren application

Decision pending. Two paths:

- **Don't switch the frame mid-application.** Keep temporal violence as the distinctive contribution; add a brief gesture in Q4 or the AI paragraph that names normative gravity as a mechanism the book traces across registers.
- **Switch the frame to normative gravity.** Rework Q4 (and the synthesis paragraph and abstract) around it. Higher cost, potentially higher payoff.

May 1 deadline argues for the first; the book's coherence over time argues for the second.

## Implication for prior published work

The Autograder bias paper (in development, "Output format as activation function") and the Contested Identities article are theorizing the same mechanism without yet recognizing it as the same. A future paper or chapter could explicitly name normative gravity as a generalizable mechanism that travels across:

- AI/probabilistic systems (Autograder, Reframe research)
- Recognition apparatus (Contested Identities, Recognition and Sentience)
- Trans embodiment under legal-administrative recognition (Anti-Trans Laws / "Weaponized Care")
- Coalitional politics (Praxis of Otherwise, lateral dynamics within coalitions)

This may be the throughline of June's research program — not distinct projects in different fields, but one mechanism described at five different scales of organization.
