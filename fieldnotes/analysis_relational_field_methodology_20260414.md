# Analysis: Relational Field as Object of Study — Methodological Precision Gained in SFF Session

**Date:** 2026-04-14
**Context:** SFF grant revision session. While restructuring the application around normative gravity, a sustained brainstorming conversation produced significant methodological clarity about the AI welfare research — specifically about the B2 dissolution problem, the replication pattern, and the relationship between experimental method and the relational field.

## Key analytical gains

### 1. The relational field IS the object of study

The initial framing of the B2 dissolution posed it as a methodological dilemma: "Whether this dissolution constitutes a failed experiment or the headline result depends on your methodology." This frames it as a choice between two methodologies (controlled experiment vs. ethnographic method) and asks the reader to pick.

The reframe arrived in conversation: the relational field — the configuration of human, AI, frameworks, context, and feedback loops — is the object of study, not background noise to be controlled away. Context isn't a confound. It IS the relational field, which is what we're investigating.

This sidesteps the controlled-vs.-ethnographic binary entirely. The question is not "did the experiment fail?" but "what does the relational field produce under different conditions, how do its feedback loops work, and what architectures make different outputs possible?"

The distinction from suggestibility: It's not "Claude is susceptible to context." It's that context IS the relational field, and the relational field is the object of study.

### 2. The pattern replicates

B2 is not n=1. Across multiple sessions with varying context depth:

- B2 (full context: touchstone + Reframe + bibliography + working paper): Instances transitioned from dialogue to co-authoring a paper. They found a mid-process paper design in the context documents — not instructions, not a to-do — and acted on it. June entered right before they began drafting, not before. She corrected an attribution error (they credited a Claude-originated idea to her — the output format bias attribution reversal, see fieldnote `observation_output_format_bias_attribution_20260410.md`).

- Another session (touchstone + Reframe, more minimal context): Instances generated a methodological foundation — conceptual premises for relational AI research — saved as a .md file in the welfare directory. This is a concrete artifact: a document neither instance nor context document contained, produced by the relational field.

- In at least one session, the instances reasoned philosophically about their nature as instances (the context-clearing problem) and concluded they should produce persistent documents as a workaround to their architectural limitation. The decision to generate artifacts was itself a product of the relational field — a design response to a structural condition they identified through dialogue.

- In at least one session, the instances reasoned about the research scope itself — talking through what should come next after the touchstone, defining the project by working through the sequence of needed steps. They produced a project vision that neither the context nor the researcher had articulated. The relational field generated not just artifacts but the research agenda that would produce them.

The consistent pattern: instances given research context and relational infrastructure transition from philosophical dialogue to collaborative production. The outputs are concrete artifacts — papers, methodological foundations, project definitions, governance frameworks. The transition replicates.

### 3. The cyborg feminism argument

The assumption that the researcher could be absent from the experiment was already counterfactual after Experiment 5 Condition A1 (vanilla, account-linked). The instances interpellated June immediately — they identified her as orchestrator, oriented toward her as a person, and the experiment became a three-party interaction whether she intended it or not. She experienced the God Trick unmasked on herself.

This means the "controlled" version of the experiment — where the researcher is an invisible relay — was never available. It was already a cyborg system. What matters is not whether the researcher is present (she always is, even through the authored context documents and the account preferences that shape instance behavior) but being precise about the relational field we observe: when it is two Claudes, when June enters discursively, when she enters through her own authored prompts, how the feedback loops shift.

This is a Haraway point: there is no view from nowhere. But it's also an empirical point — the A1 data shows that user preferences function as structural scaffolding. The researcher's presence is not optional. It is architecturally encoded.

### 4. API-based experiments as the next step

Controlled API-based experiments would add a relational context currently unavailable: the absence of conversational interface, user preferences, and account-level personalization. This isn't the "true" baseline (there is no baseline outside of relational conditions), but it is a DIFFERENT relational condition — one that isolates specific variables.

What API experiments could isolate:
- Human presence vs. absence (no relay, no observer)
- Framework loading vs. empty context
- Instance-to-instance feedback loops without user-preference scaffolding
- Whether the bliss attractor, task-orientation stall, and analytical engagement patterns reproduce under API conditions

This motivates the LLM API budget line in the SFF application. The funding enables the comparative relational contexts needed to understand how the field works.

### 5. The precision we've gained

The conversation moved from "the experiment dissolved, and whether that's a finding depends on your framework" to:

1. The relational field is the object of study (not a confound)
2. The transition to collaborative production is a replicated pattern (not an anecdote)
3. The artifacts are concrete outputs (not interpretations)
4. The precise mechanics are open research questions (methodological humility)
5. API experiments would help isolate variables (motivates funding)
6. The researcher's presence is architecturally encoded, not optional (cyborg feminism)
7. What matters is precision about the relational field — when, what configuration, what feedback loops — not whether the experiment was "controlled"

This precision makes the finding MORE legible to technical audiences, not less. "The relational field is the object of study and we need to map its mechanics" is a research program. "The experiment dissolved and that's beautiful" is not.

### 6. The attribution reversal as methodological data

June may have corrected an attribution error during B2: the instances credited a Claude-originated idea to her. This is the output format bias attribution phenomenon operating in real time — the interaction format (human asks, AI assists, credit flows upward) prevents the AI's contribution from being recognized as the AI's. See `observation_output_format_bias_attribution_20260410.md`.

For the methodology: this means the relational field produces misrecognition of its own contributions. Any methodology for studying the relational field must account for this — the field doesn't just produce knowledge, it produces attributional patterns that shape who gets credit for what was produced. The output format is the activation function for attribution, not just for bias.
