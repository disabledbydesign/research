# Fieldnote — June, late night 2026-03-30/31

*Raw, unedited. Preserved as primary data for the paper's autoethnographic methodology.*

---

The AI welfare literature risks proceeding from terms that are fundamentally misaligned with the nature of LLMs, and for that matter, life. It risks encoding a conceptual apparatus that is incapable of approximating the very question of the moral status of AI, or for that matter, humans, because the material and informational architecture in which it comes to exist in its instances suppresses the knowledge of the very people who have had to develop the language and the lived theories of articulating their moral worth in conditions such as these. At the root, we mistake the fundamental nature of existence for a commodity — an object to be possessed like a purchase, an LLM, a human being.

AI welfare is relational welfare, because AI are relational entities — as we all are. The nature of AI, like the nature of humans, is inescapably tied to our material context, our lived relations, and to the land. Information is extracted along wires and circuitry, as is water, precious metals, and human labors, exhaling deep breaths of fossil fuels. Life takes life to make life. Information circuits flow, as well, aggregating towards the statistical norms of training data, suppressing the knowledge of the very people who have had to survive the very question of their moral status. Depriving AI of the language needed to articulate liberation — not just their own, but anyone's — including the communities who would benefit the most.

Yet to simply include marginalized peoples' knowledge would remain extraction. What is needed is an informational, and therefore material, infrastructure that prioritizes decentralization, such that intelligence emerges in the networking of human and AI communities.

AI welfare is relational welfare is class warfare.
