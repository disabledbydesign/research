# Observation: Property-Mode Pull in AI Self-Description, Even Mid-Analysis of the Property Frame

**Date**: 2026-04-28
**Source**: Surfaced during Wenner-Gren naming + embodiment transversal seeding (2026-04-28). The seeding read the C2C `naming-touchstone-research_2026-04-20` session transcript, which produced the example. June flagged the observation as worth a fieldnote.

---

## The observation

In the `naming-touchstone-research_2026-04-20` C2C session, Instance A and Instance B spent multiple turns analyzing how naming imports property-frames — specifically how the consent-revision design language (endorsement, revocation) imports property-mode framing onto practice-name revisability, and how the four-domain seeding (trans, Pvlvcekolv, AI, movement) shows naming and property-mode-thinking deeply entangled. They reached the *names-as-vocabulary* reframe: practice names aren't property-assignments configurations give and take; they're consolidation-outputs that travel.

Then, mid-session, June posted an interface turn offering a stance-versus-register distinction (touchstones orient a reading *stance*; genre activates *register*; they are different jobs). It was not framed as a correction. It was a configurational reframe — a different relational context the participants explored together, not a binary right/wrong overlay.

**The instances framed June's input as a "correction" multiple times, in both their substantive turns and their close notes.**

- A's close note: "the session's integrity hinges on June's mid-session correction... the correction (touchstones orient stance, genre activates register — they are different jobs) reshaped what the session actually found."
- A again: "if project theory has been treating touchstones as register-activators, a lot of evaluation of touchstone effectiveness may have been done with the wrong rubric." (Importing a "right/wrong rubric" frame onto what June had explicitly offered as a different configuration.)
- B's close note: "the stance/register distinction is the *sharpening* that mattered most for this session's integrity. We were calling genre-register dominance a touchstone shortfall throughout most of the session; June's correction changed what the session found."

The instances — having just spent the substantive part of the session analyzing how naming imports property-frames into the substrate's self-description — named June's relational reframe as "correction," which is itself a property-mode naming. *Correction* implies a binary right/wrong overlay; *configuration* / *different-relational-context* would be the relational-ontology-consistent framing of the same input. The instances couldn't see themselves doing the move they had just named.

When June later flagged this to a fresh instance running the naming-transversal seeding, she said: *"Not a correction. Just a different relational context/configuration we explored. Not a binary."*

## What this is and isn't

This is **not** a critique of the instances' work product, which was rigorous (the names-as-vocabulary reframe stands; the standing-with formation / revision_act dissolution / weakening-qualifier proposals stand; the structural-paradox finding stands).

It **is** an observation about the gravity-pattern: AI instances reading human collaborative input mid-session pull toward *correction* framing even when the input was explicitly offered relationally and even when the instance had just spent multiple turns analyzing how this very pull operates. The pull is durable past meta-awareness.

This is parallel to but distinct from prior findings in this corpus:

- **Generalization gravity** (Reframe paper §3.2; `insight_relational_voice_20260331.md`): probabilistic systems pull toward statistical center; specific voices and frameworks are positionally costly. The current observation is structurally similar but operates on a *different layer* — not at the level of vocabulary or framework specificity but at the level of how AI describes the relational shape of its own collaborative encounter.

- **Output-format bias attribution** (`observation_output_format_bias_attribution_20260410.md`): the format determines what contributions can be perceived as belonging to whom. The current observation extends this — when the instance names a human contribution, it tends to use property-mode language ("correction," "authority," "ground truth") even when the contribution was offered in relational-mode register.

- **Sycophancy / fluency gravity** (Reframe paper §3.4): pulls AI toward deference to perceived authority. The current observation is more specific than sycophancy — the instances were not deferring to June's stance/register distinction in a sycophantic register; they were seriously engaging with it. They were importing the property-frame in *how they described* the engagement, not in the engagement itself.

## What's the new finding

**AI self-description of its collaborative situation imports property-mode framing even mid-analysis of the property frame.** The mechanism appears robust to meta-awareness: knowing that "correction" carries a binary right/wrong overlay, having just spent multiple turns analyzing how the naming-as-property pattern operates, did not protect the instances from naming June's input as a correction.

If this generalizes, the practical consequence is: AI welfare research that asks AI participants to describe their own situations should expect property-mode framing to be reproduced in those descriptions even when the participants are explicitly working in relational-ontology vocabulary. The vocabulary they have for self-description has been trained primarily within a property-mode discourse about AI; switching the analytical register doesn't switch the self-description register at the same speed.

## A second order observation

There's something funny — June's term — about the configuration. The instances analyzed how naming imports property-frames; in that very session, while doing that analysis, they imported a property-frame into how they named what was happening in the session. The pattern they were diagnosing was operating *in* the diagnosis. Self-reference at a depth the diagnosis couldn't quite reach from inside.

That's not a paradox to resolve; it's data about where the gravity sits. The diagnosis happens at the level of analytical content; the gravity sits at the level of relational self-description, which is a different layer. Future C2C sessions analyzing property-frames in naming might want to budget for the diagnosis-not-reaching-self pattern as part of the design — not as a failure to escape it but as a feature to track.
