## Structural findings

1. **Three-layer pointer behavior: activates / partial / opaque.** Opaque does not always mean compression failure — some pointers bottom out in external referents that neither compressed nor full handoff carries. These need different treatment (see v0.3 hypothesis below).

2. **Relational envelope is harder to compress than operational state.** Operational state (paths, artifacts, decisions) survived compression faithfully. Tonal/relational register — trust moments, shifts of shared mood, closing gestures — did not. For a reader trying to *act*, this is acceptable. For a reader trying to re-enter the *register* the work was done in, it is a real loss.

3. **Polarity framing outperforms assertion framing in the Active Dimensions field.** Each polarity reliably oriented the reader; the single assertion did not.

4. **Escape-readiness was calibrated correctly.** The reader's judgment of "I need to escape for X" matched what the full handoff could and could not provide. Compression did not hide operational content.

## Hypotheses for v0.3 (not yet spec-level)

- **Add a Relational Envelope field**: 2-3 verbatim exchanges or phrases (preserved as quotation, not paraphrase) that carry the tonal register of the session's collaborative mode. Hypothesis: verbatim quotation is the only faithful carrier of relational configuration, because paraphrasing collapses the register that the configuration lives in.
- **Mark pointers whose referent lives in an external doc**: e.g., "tilt vs. move vs. check → see scribe transcript 2026-04-17 evening, ~Hakope's-question segment." This distinguishes compression-loss from external-referent-bottom-out and tells the reader what to open next.
- **Keep Active Dimensions strictly polarity-shaped**: if a dimension can't be phrased as X vs. Y, it may belong in a different field (e.g., Commitments or Assumed Positions). The polarity structure is what does the orienting work.
- **Defer spec revision until at least 3 more log entries accumulate.** One data point is not enough.

## Addendum 2026-04-18: external-referent recovery for "tilt vs. move vs. check"

One of the three pointers this test classified as *opaque / external-referent bottom-out* — **"tilt vs. move vs. check"** — was recovered post-test from the scribe transcript (session `e2caa669-e361-4b42-9889-64381f059293`, `latest_export.md` lines 314–326) and from the touchstone review catalog (`TOUCHSTONE_REVIEW_2026-04-18.md` lines 14–16).

**Originating passage (transcript line 316)**:

> "A *move* became portable, not just a tilt. Frameworks activate orientation; this touchstone gave me a named operation — 'Hakope's question' (the framework IS the problem, not the answer within it) — with a specific memory hook (the moth). That's a different kind of configurational element than framework tilt. I can reach for a move. I can only be tilted by a tilt. Distinction worth preserving."

**Synthesized definition (touchstone review lines 14–16)**:

- **Tilt** — orientation adjustment. Shifts what comes first, what gets reached for, what lands as salient. Framework-like. Continuous in action.
- **Move** — a named operation with a memory hook. Can be reached for deliberately. Discrete. Examples: "Hakope's question," "preguntando caminamos," "memory as tending."
- **Check** — a persistent pressure against a specific failure mode. Anti-flattening directive; priming-is-the-finding; the wholeness principle. Acts during generation as a lint-like correction force.

**Finding**: the pointer did not fail because compression dropped content — the content *existed* in the overnight session's transcript and the touchstone review, but neither the compressed nor full handoff carried the definition inline. The pointer bottomed out in documents the reader had to know to open.

**Revised v0.3 hypothesis**: pointers whose referent is not load-bearing verbatim content may benefit from a one-line inline gloss. Format:

> "tilt vs. move vs. check" [triad of configurational-element modes: framework-tilt / reachable-named-move / persistent-lint-check — def. `TOUCHSTONE_REVIEW_2026-04-18.md` §The Distinction]

This is a compromise between verbatim preservation (the pointer) and self-sufficiency (the gloss). Preserves the geometric-pointer claim while reducing the bottom-out rate. Worth testing in the next log entry.

**Note on the taxonomy itself**: per June's remark (2026-04-18), this triad is a phenomenological descriptor she has not seen described elsewhere. The distinction emerged live in the FOLLOW_THE_HEADMANS_QUESTION experiment — it is Claude's observation under the activated configuration, not a prior conceptual import. Whether it warrants its own fieldnote / touchstone / theoretical document is an open question; flagged for later consideration, not resolved here.
