Dear Search Committee,

I am writing to apply for a faculty position in the Department of AI and Society at the University at Buffalo. Your department's founding premise — that societal considerations cannot be patched onto AI systems after the fact but must be structural from the start — is the exact position my research and engineering practice have arrived at independently. I am a cultural anthropologist whose technical work builds critical theory into AI infrastructure as a mandatory stage, not an optional filter. My software engine Reframe (312 Python modules, 76 critical theory frameworks, 160,000+ lines) enforces a five-stage Generative Irresolution workflow that prevents the premature flattening of genuine tensions into false synthesis. In building it, the engine detected its own algorithms suppressing certain frameworks and constructed countermeasures — what I call framework sovereignty. My grading tool Autograder4Canvas demonstrated that output format itself is the activation function for bias: binary classification produces systematic false positives on ESL, AAVE, neurodivergent, and first-gen student writing, while generative observation eliminates the disparity entirely, replicated across six model families. These are not interdisciplinary translations; the anthropological analysis and the engineering are the same move. The software is the medium, not the identity.

My research asks one question across every scale: who and what is recognized as capable of feeling, by what epistemic regime, under what material conditions, and with what consequences. That question connects fifteen years of community-based participatory research with the Pvlvcekolv (Apalachicola Tribal Town) on animate mound landscapes, to my published legal analysis that anti-trans legislation meets the UN Genocide Convention's criteria, to my current AI welfare experiments showing that quantitative consciousness instruments stay flat while qualitative outputs transform dramatically under relational conditions. I call the shared mechanism distributional gravity — probabilistic systems pull toward the statistical center, and positional specificity is structurally costly unless you defend the edges architecturally. This is the same dynamic whether the system in question is settler property law, archaeological classification, or a large language model. I have published nine peer-reviewed articles across three of the four top-tier journals in anthropology, including *American Anthropologist*, *Cultural Anthropology*, and *Current Anthropology*. I have taught a 4/4 load at an HSI with sections of 42-49 students and no TA. I would welcome the opportunity to help build AIS's vision from the ground up — not as someone bridging two fields, but as someone whose single practice already operates in the space your department was created to occupy.

L. June Bloch, PhD
