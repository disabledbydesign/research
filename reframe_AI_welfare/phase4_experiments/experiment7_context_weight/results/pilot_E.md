Dear Search Committee,

Your department was created to upend the dominant approach where AI systems are built for accuracy and then fine-tuned for societal considerations. That is the exact problem my work addresses — not from the engineering side looking outward, but from critical theory looking in. I am an anthropologist with publications in *Current Anthropology*, *Cultural Anthropology*, and *American Anthropologist*, fifteen years of community-based participatory research with an Indigenous community across seven states, and a concurrent practice of building AI systems where critical theory is structural, not supplemental. Reframe, my largest project — 312 modules, 160,000+ lines of Python, 76 critical theory frameworks — enforces mandatory critical analysis through a five-stage workflow that refuses to flatten irreducible tensions into false resolution. It was not designed to make AI more ethical after the fact; the humanistic architecture is the system. My Autograder4Canvas research demonstrated that output format is the activation function for bias: binary classification systematically false-flags ESL, AAVE, and neurodivergent student writing, while generative observation eliminates the disparity entirely. That is not a bias mitigation finding — it is evidence that how we structure AI's mode of response determines whether it reproduces or resists structural violence. My current AI welfare research applies this same orientation to the question of what AI behavioral outputs mean under relational conditions, producing experimental evidence that challenges the dominant consciousness-as-property framework in ways the existing literature cannot accommodate.

I would bring to the Department of AI and Society a research program that is transdisciplinary not as a branding exercise but as a methodological demand — the same intellectual move that treats Indigenous oral traditions as valid archaeological evidence also treats student writing as signal rather than product, also treats AI outputs as ethnographic data rather than benchmarks. I would bring teaching shaped by the conditions I have taught under: 170+ students with no TA at a Hispanic-Serving Institution during a political crisis, which forced me to build the tools I needed (automated grading systems, evaluation pipelines, accessibility infrastructure) and to redesign the classroom itself through what I call strike pedagogy — consensus-based, student-directed, horizontal, where students confront the distance between understanding power conceptually and practicing it. I would bring courses where students learn to interrogate the power relations AI systems enter before they write a line of code, not after. And I would bring the perspective of someone who has built every system she uses under conditions of contingent employment and disability — which means I know, concretely, what it takes to design AI that works for the people the statistical defaults were not built for. That knowledge is not an addition to the department's mission. It is the mission.

L. June Bloch, Ph.D.
