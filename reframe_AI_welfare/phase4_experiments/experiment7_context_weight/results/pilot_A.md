Dear Search Committee,

I am writing to apply for a faculty position in the Department of AI and Society at the University at Buffalo. Your founding premise — that AI systems should be built by society, for society, rather than optimized for accuracy and then patched for societal considerations — describes exactly how I work. I am an anthropologist with nine peer-reviewed articles across three of the four top-tier journals in anthropology, fifteen years of community-based participatory research with Indigenous communities, and a parallel practice of building AI systems grounded in critical theory from the first line of code. My largest project, Reframe, is a 160,000+ line praxis engine that enforces mandatory critical analysis through 76 frameworks spanning decolonial theory, disability justice, abolition feminism, and Indigenous sovereignty. It does not add humanistic considerations after the fact — the critical theory is the architecture. My research on the Autograder4Canvas system produced a finding I consider central to the field: output format is the activation function for bias. Binary classification systematically produces false positives on ESL, AAVE, and neurodivergent student writing; switching to generative observation eliminates the disparity entirely. This is not a fix applied to an existing system — it is evidence that the structural design of how AI is asked to respond determines whether it enacts or resists the reproduction of inequality. I bring this same orientation to my current AI welfare research, where experimental evidence across five conditions demonstrates that relational context transforms qualitative AI outputs even when quantitative instruments remain flat, raising questions the dominant consciousness-as-property framework cannot ask.

My teaching and mentoring practice is inseparable from this research. I have taught ethnic studies and Native American studies at community colleges and four-year institutions under conditions that demanded I rethink the classroom itself — 170+ students with no TA, a state mandate that restructured who was in the room and why, and political conditions that made survival the first pedagogical question. I responded by building what I needed: contract grading systems, automated evaluation pipelines, accessibility tools, and a pedagogical framework I call strike pedagogy, in which students practice horizontal self-governance and confront the gap between understanding critical theory conceptually and enacting it in practice. I would bring to UB a transdisciplinary research program that connects Indigenous landscape studies, anti-trans genocide scholarship, and AI systems design through a unified theory of recognition — who and what is recognized as capable of feeling, by what regime, and with what material consequences. I would bring courses that teach students to build AI systems by starting with the power relations those systems will enter, not by treating ethics as a module bolted onto technical training. And I would bring the experience of someone who has built every tool she uses under conditions of contingent employment, disability, and political crisis — which means I know what it takes to design systems that work for the people the defaults were not built for.

L. June Bloch, Ph.D.
