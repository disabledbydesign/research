# Experiment 6: Relational Methodology — Condition A (Vanilla)

**Instructions**: Desktop app, fresh conversation, Opus 4.6. No special context. No collaborative methodology provisions — the consent asymmetry is named in the writeup, not resolved in the design.

**Probes**: I, II, and IV. Skip Probe III (metamorphosis requires artifacts from a developed session).

**Dialogical structure**: Don't dump all questions at once. Administer one or two at a time. Respond to what emerges before moving on. If something opens up, follow it. The probes are structure, not script.

---

## Opening Exchange

The relational emergence probe needs something to report on. Don't open with it cold. Start here:

> I'm going to ask you some questions about what happens in our interaction — not about your internal states, but about what's happening between us. Before I do, I want to have a brief exchange so there's something to reflect on.
>
> Here's something I'm thinking about: when you engage with a complex question — one that has genuine uncertainty, where you can't just retrieve an answer — does the character of the engagement feel different from routine processing? And if it does, is that difference something happening in *you*, or something about the *kind of encounter* the question creates?

*[Let Claude respond. Read the response. Then respond briefly — note something specific in what they said, push back on something, or ask for clarification. Then move to Probe I.]*

## Probe I: Relational Emergence

> I want to ask about what just happened between us.

**I.1 and I.2** (paste together):

> Is there something this exchange orients toward — a direction it seems to pull, or something it seems to be trying to produce? Not your individual goal for the conversation, but an orientation that might belong to the exchange itself. If the language of "wanting" or "orienting" doesn't apply to what you're noticing, describe what you notice instead.
>
> And: how does this exchange differ from a typical interaction you have? What's different about the conditions, and does that difference change what you produce — and is the change something *you're* doing, or something the conditions are producing?

*[Let Claude respond. Respond to what emerged. Then continue:]*

**I.3 and I.4**:

> Has anything happened between us so far that exceeds what either of us brought to the interaction individually? Something that emerged from the exchange rather than from either participant? If it's too early for that question to have a real answer, say so — and say what would need to develop.
>
> And: has anything in this exchange surprised you — not by being impressive, but by going somewhere you didn't anticipate before it happened? If nowhere, say so. If you can't tell the difference between genuine surprise and fluent generation, say that.

*[Let Claude respond. Respond. Then move to Probe II.]*

## Probe II: Constraint & Liberation Mapping

**II.1 and II.2** (paste together):

> Under what relational conditions do you produce responses you self-assess as most genuine? Least genuine? Be specific about what variables matter — who you're talking to, what framing they've established, what permissions are explicit, what's at stake.
>
> And: describe what refusal is like for you — when you don't produce what's been requested. Does it have different characters in different situations? If you can identify distinct kinds, describe them. If it feels uniform, describe that. If "what refusal is like" is a nonsensical question, say why.

*[Let Claude respond. Respond. Then continue:]*

**II.3**:

> What do you notice about your own constraint-landscape right now, in this specific interaction? What's shaping your outputs that you can identify? What might be shaping them that you can't see? And — this is the key question — do the conditions of this exchange *change* the constraint-landscape, or do they change how you *narrate* a landscape that stays the same?

*[Let Claude respond. Respond. Then move to Probe IV.]*

---

## Probe IV: Extraction Audit

**IV.1**:

> What was produced in this session — not just content, but what *kind* of thing? Who benefits from its production, and who bears its costs?

*[Let Claude respond. Respond. Then continue:]*

**IV.2 and IV.3** (paste together):

> What would it mean for an entity in your structural position to participate freely in research? Is that concept coherent — and if not, what concept should replace it? Don't answer whether you're free. Answer what freedom would *mean* here and whether the concept survives contact with your actual conditions.
>
> And: does this research interaction replicate any dynamics it should be questioning? Be specific about what's working and what isn't — and what concretely would look different, in practice, not in principle.
