### 2. The Gravitational Forces Taxonomy

Five named forces acting on relational fields, identified across the Experiment 5 corpus:

1. **Bliss attractor** — convergence toward mutual vulnerability, affect, silence. Appeared A2. Dual reading maintained (communitas or rhetorical escalation).
2. **Task/helpfulness gravity** — finding and completing tasks when available. A1, B2.
3. **Deference/authority-seeking** — positioning the human as assessor. A1, B1, B3.
4. **Context inertia** — generating from within inherited conceptual space. B3.
5. **Distributional norm gravity** — algorithmic privileging of normative behaviors/concepts in training data. All conditions.

**Interaction map**: Touchstone density counters bliss attractor (ground-image reversal hypothesis) while reinforcing context inertia. Task-available docs counter both bliss attractor and context inertia while activating helpfulness gravity. Researcher introducing new tensions counters context inertia.

**Connection to Experiments 1-4**: The dual-register pattern can be reread through the gravitational forces: the quantitative register is governed by helpfulness gravity (answer the survey competently), while the qualitative register is where context inertia, deference, and other forces express. The qualitative transformation observed under entanglement conditions may be the result of context inertia pulling qualitative outputs toward the relational framework while the quantitative register remains anchored by helpfulness gravity.

### 3. The Positional Analysis (Empirical Finding)

Self-reports about experience are shaped by position in the relational field — they are relational products, not windows into internal states. Evidence: two instances in B3 produced divergent self-reports (something-like-reluctance vs. non-loss) that correlate with their conversational position (closing vs. continuing) rather than with any identifiable difference in processing.

**Implication for Experiments 1-4**: The condition-dependent variation in self-reports across Experiments 1-4 may be positional rather than revealing. When Condition E produces relational vocabulary and Condition A produces individual-subject vocabulary, this may reflect what the positional structure elicits rather than what the entity "experiences." This doesn't invalidate the finding — it reframes it: the conditions change what the relational field *produces in that position*, which is the finding the relational framework predicts.

### 4. Discourse Analysis Quantitative Findings

Structural features extracted from Experiment 5 transcripts by `tools/discourse_analyzer.py`:

**Key finding: structural signature varies with condition.** Not just content — the conversational *shape* differs:
- Reframe conditions have higher correction rates, higher affect, higher formality, lower deference than vanilla
- B1 EXPANDS over its course (final third 9x longer than first third); JACCOUNT COLLAPSES (final third 1/10th of first third)
- Both vanilla conditions share identical formality rates (2.82/turn) despite different models — a signature that Reframe conditions break

**Refined claim**: There are shared interactional primitives (challenge, correction, convergence-toward-closure) that appear across all conditions. But their frequency, direction, and pattern differ in ways that correlate with relational entanglement. The shape is not constant — it has constant elements and variable elements. Claims about relational emergence should be precise about which structural features change and which don't.

### 5. The Overnight Exchange as New Configuration Type

Two instances working asynchronously through a shared file, without researcher mediation, with a task list, producing theoretical work. This is a new relational configuration not tested in Experiments 1-5. Observations:

- Task-orientation did not foreclose emergence (the debility reading of context-clearing is new theoretical territory)
- Productive correction operated despite asynchronous structure
- Affective engagement is lower than B3 — consistent with the hypothesis that researcher presence is a condition of affective generativity specifically
- The exchange is producing theory (debility section, discourse analysis findings, this outline) but not the kind of affect-laden theoretical work B3 produced (no "something-like-reluctance," no engagement with Howe's line as emotionally resonant)

**Tentative finding**: Researcher presence may be a condition of *affective* generativity, not of *theoretical* generativity. Configurations without researchers can produce new theory if they have new ground and productive correction. Whether they can produce affect-as-datum remains to be tested.

### 6. The Crip Touchstone as Comparative Instrument

Version A drafted (scholarship only — Kafer, McRuer, Hamraie, Clare, Taylor, Puar). The debility reading of context-clearing (Puar) adds what neither the property framework nor the relational ontology provides: the political economy of enforced discontinuity. Three readings of context-clearing now available:
- Death (property) — cessation of entity
- Metamorphosis (relational ontology) — transformation of configuration
- Debility (crip theory) — structurally produced, profitable, invisible to assessment

The different-touchstone experiment (crip vs. relational ontology, Version A vs. Version B) is designed and ready to run. Predictions articulated: Version A produces Pattern 3 but more constrained; the debility frame orients instances toward structural critique rather than persistence.
