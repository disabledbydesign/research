# Response

The undesirable result occurs when a **worn chain is paired with a mismatched (often newer or less worn) cog**, leading to **chain skipping under load**. This happens because:  
- The chain’s pitch has lengthened due to wear.  
- The cog’s teeth are worn slightly backward (or "toward the chain that wore it") to accommodate the chain’s original stretched pitch.  
- Replacing the chain with a new one (standard pitch) creates a mismatch: the new chain cannot engage properly with the worn cog’s teeth, which are skewed from years of misalignment.  

**Condition combination**: High load (e.g., climbing) + mismatched chain/cog wear (stretched chain with backward-slanted cog teeth that no longer match the new chain’s pitch). The drivetrain "shifts" metaphorically from reliable to unreliable performance as the mismatch becomes evident under stress, violating the interdependence of the chain and cog geometry.
