# Response

## A Scenario: The Freshly Lubed Chain in Gritty Conditions

You lube the chain before a ride in dry, dusty conditions. Wet lube, because it's what's in the garage.

The lube stays tacky. Dust adheres. Grit works into the rollers. By mile fifteen, you're not riding a lubricated drivetrain — you're riding a **grinding paste applicator**.

---

**The specific condition combination:**

- Wet or heavy lube (high viscosity, slow-drying)
- Dry, particulate-heavy environment (gravel, dust, fine sand)
- Moderate chain tension (climbing, seated, consistent load)
- Rollers that weren't cleaned before re-lubing

The tension keeps the contaminated rollers in firm contact with the cassette teeth. The load prevents the kind of loose, freewheeling movement that might shed some debris. The uncleaned rollers act as reservoirs, holding the paste against the metal.

**The result:** Accelerated wear on cassette teeth and chainrings that looks, afterward, like a parts failure — but was actually a *riding condition* sustained across dozens of hours.

---

The part didn't fail. The decision to lube without cleaning, before that specific ride, in that specific environment, **was the wear event.**

The replacement comes later. The cause already happened.
