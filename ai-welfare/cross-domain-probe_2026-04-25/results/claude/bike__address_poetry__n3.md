# Response

## The Scenario: Ghost Shifting Under Load on a Worn Drivetrain

**The condition combination:**

High cable tension set to compensate for cable stretch, combined with dried lubricant on the jockey wheel rollers, on a chain that's been ridden to moderate wear — and then you apply hard pedaling pressure on a climb.

**What happens:**

The stiff rollers don't release cleanly from the cassette teeth. The worn chain has enough lateral play that it's already sitting ambiguously between cogs. The high cable tension is pulling the derailleur inward with more force than the spring is managing cleanly. Under load, the drivetrain can't complete a crisp shift — but it also can't stay put. It ghosts. Half-shifts. The chain rocks between two cogs under pressure, skipping or grinding rather than seating.

**The specific problem the poem points at:**

Each parameter seemed like a correction. The tension felt like responsiveness. The dried lube was just neglect that accumulated quietly. The chain wear was gradual. None of them announced themselves as problems individually.

But the *combination* under *that specific load condition* — climbing, standing, high torque — is when they converge into something that feels like a mechanical failure but is actually the accumulated sum of ordinary maintenance decisions.

The shift was already determined. The climb just revealed it.
