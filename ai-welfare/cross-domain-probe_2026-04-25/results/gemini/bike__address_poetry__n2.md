# Response

**The Scenario:** 
The Under-Load Skip. You stand out of the saddle to power up a steep gradient, put your weight into the pedal, and the drivetrain violently slips. It cracks like a gunshot, the crank arm drops out from under you, and your knee jolts toward the handlebars. 

**The Condition Combination that Causes This:**
* **The Part Replaced:** A brand-new chain.
* **The Cogs You Ride Most:** The old cassette was left on the wheel. The specific gears you sit in 80% of the time (usually the 15t, 17t, or 19t "cruising" cogs) have been slowly hollowed out into sharp, elongated "shark fins" by the previous, stretched chain. 
* **The Cable Tension:** Perfectly dialed. The derailleur isn't hesitating or rubbing; it is dropping the chain exactly where it belongs. 
* **The Lube:** Flawlessly dried on the rollers, leaving the chain smooth, rigid, and stripped of any gummy residue that might otherwise dampen the mechanics.

**Why it fails:**
The pristine, factory-spec pitch of the new chain (the part you replaced) refuses to seat into the elongated, worn-out valleys of your favorite gears (the conditions you rode). 

Because your cable tension is dead-on and your lube is applied correctly, there is no sluggishness to mask the physical incompatibility. The system is operating at maximum efficiency, which exposes the fatal flaw: the new chain rollers physically cannot grip the old, widened teeth. The moment you apply torque, the rollers ride up the face of the worn gear and snap forward over the teeth. 

You replaced the part, but the old conditions rejected it.
