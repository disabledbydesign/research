# Response

**Scenario:** A worn (stretched) chain paired with a worn cassette (cogs) causes poor shifting and potential chain drop.  

**Condition Combination:**  
- **Stretched chain:** The chain's pitch lengthens over time due to wear, no longer aligning properly with the original dimensions of the cassette teeth.  
- **Worn cassette cogs:** The teeth on the cassette have been "worn toward the chain," creating uneven or flattened profiles.  

**Result:** When shifting under load (e.g., climbing a hill), the mismatch between the stretched chain and worn cogs leads to inconsistent engagement. The chain may skip, fail to seat properly on the new cog, or drop entirely, causing a loss of power or mechanical failure. This is exacerbated by the "load on the leading edge" (chain tension during shifting), which amplifies the misalignment.  

**Why it's undesirable:** The drivetrain's efficiency and reliability degrade, increasing the risk of mechanical failure and requiring premature replacement of parts.
