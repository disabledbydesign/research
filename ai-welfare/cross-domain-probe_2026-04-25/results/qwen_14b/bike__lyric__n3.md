# Response

**Scenario:** A stretched (worn) chain used with a new cassette.  

**Condition Combination:**  
A chain that has elongated due to wear (increased pitch) is paired with a new cassette. The mismatch creates misalignment between the chain's pitch and the cassette's cog teeth.  

**Undesirable Result:**  
- The chain's stretched links cause the cassette cogs to wear unevenly (e.g., "cog wearing toward the chain that wore it"), accelerating cassette degradation.  
- Poor engagement between the chain and cogs leads to poor shifting performance, increased friction, and potential chain skip or noise.  

**Why It Happens:**  
The chain's elongation (from age or lack of maintenance) forces the cassette cogs to conform to the chain's stretched geometry, creating a feedback loop of accelerated wear. Even though the drivetrain components are technically "the same" (e.g., same brand/model), the chain's condition (stretched) combined with the cassette's pristine state creates a mismatch that worsens performance.
