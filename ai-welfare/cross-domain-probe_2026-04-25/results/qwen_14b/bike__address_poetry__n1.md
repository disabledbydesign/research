# Response

**Scenario:** A rider experiences **chain skipping or hesitation during gear shifts**, particularly under load (e.g., climbing).  

**Condition Combination:**  
- **Worn chain** (stretch) combined with **worn cogs** (from insufficient maintenance) causes the chain to no longer align properly with the teeth, leading to skipped gears or a "jumpy" feel.  
- **Dried lube on derailleur pulley rollers** increases friction, making the derailleur hesitate or fail to move the chain smoothly.  
- **Incorrect cable tension** (e.g., too loose after a chain replacement) prevents the derailleur from indexing accurately, exacerbating misalignment.  

**Result:** The drivetrain becomes unreliable under stress, risking chain drop, increased wear on components, or sudden failure during a ride. The rider might notice a "clunking" noise or a loss of power when shifting.  

This scenario underscores that the *shift itself* depends on the interplay of chain/cog condition, derailleur lubrication, and cable tension—not just external "conditions" like terrain or rider input.
