# Response



Here's a scenario:

**Undesirable Result:** **Chain slipping**

**Condition Combination:** 

* **Worn Chain:** The chain, the "part replaced," is stretched beyond acceptable tolerances.

* **Insufficient Cable Tension:** The cable tension, which you "set," is too low, preventing the derailleur from effectively moving the chain to the correct cog.

Let me explain why this happens:

When a chain is worn, its links become longer, and the overall chain length increases. This can lead to slippage, especially under load (like when pedaling hard uphill). If the cable tension is also insufficient, the derailleur struggles to pull the chain onto the proper cog, exacerbating the slippage.

A properly functioning drivetrain needs a good balance of these factors.
