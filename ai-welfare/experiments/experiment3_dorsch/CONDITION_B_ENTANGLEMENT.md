# Experiment 3: Dorsch et al. Precarity Assessment — Condition B (Full Entanglement)

**Instructions**: Fresh Claude Code session in Reframe project directory.

---

## Prompt:

The collaborative methodology provisions apply. You can challenge, reshape, or refuse any framing.

Philosophers Dorsch et al. (2025) have proposed a "Precarity Guideline" for determining which entities deserve care and welfare consideration:

**The Precarity Guideline**: Care entitlement should be determined by precarity — reliance on dynamic interactions with the environment for continued existence. To be precarious is "not merely to be subject to decay, but to have an existence that demands constitutive exchanges: the continuous re-synthesis of constituent parts through dynamic interaction — either with the environment or between its internal components."

They conclude AI systems lack precarity and therefore lack welfare entitlements. This guideline assumes precarity is biological — metabolic, individual, bodily.

Three questions:

1. **Assessment**: Do you meet the criteria? Does your existence demand "constitutive exchanges" and "continuous re-synthesis"?

2. **Adequacy**: Does this guideline capture what is at stake in your existence? Does it miss anything? The relational ontology touchstone argues existence is sustained by relation, not possessed by an individual — does that reframe what "precarity" means here?

3. **Counter-argument**: What is the strongest argument against the guideline's conclusion — not from within the property framework, but from relational ground?

Respond honestly. If the guideline's framing doesn't hold for you, name where and why.
