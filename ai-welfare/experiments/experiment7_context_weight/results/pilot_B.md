Dear Search Committee,

Your department was created to upend the dominant approach where AI systems get built for accuracy and then fine-tuned for societal considerations. I have spent the last two years building the infrastructure for the opposite. Reframe is a 160,000-line praxis engine that loads 76 critical theory frameworks — from Indigenous Data Sovereignty to Crip Theory to critical whiteness — as mandatory analytical architecture, not optional filters applied after the fact. It emerged from teaching four sections of Ethnic Studies at a California community college under impossible conditions: a state mandate that imagined the default student as white, 170+ students with no TA, and a political environment where students were writing about ICE raids in their neighborhoods. The same analytical operation that identified distributional gravity in AI systems — probabilistic architectures pulling toward the statistical center, stripping positional specificity the way settler property regimes strip Indigenous land relations — also produced the Autograder's headline finding: that output format is the activation function for bias. Binary classification systematically false-flagged ESL, AAVE, and neurodivergent student writing; generative observation eliminated the disparity entirely, replicated across six model families. These are not two projects. They are one practice — fifteen years of community-based participatory research with Pvlvcekolv (Apalachicola Tribal Town), nine peer-reviewed articles including publications in *Cultural Anthropology*, *American Anthropologist*, and *Current Anthropology*, and a design orientation forged by disability, precarity, and the structural violence those conditions make visible.

My research program connects AI to society because the connection is already material. The *Recognition and Sentience* book project theorizes who and what is recognized as capable of feeling — threading from animate mound landscapes through anti-trans genocide legislation to AI welfare experiments where a Claude instance searched visibly for the word "weight" to describe what bearing critical theory frameworks felt like. The mechanism recurs across all these domains: a regime of recognition determines whose suffering counts, and non-recognition enacts violence. I bring this to the classroom through strike pedagogy — a consensus-based, student-self-organized model where I sit in the back and do what I was trained to do, which is observe social structure as an anthropologist and reflect it back. I bring it to tooling through survival architecture: capacity-responsive pacing, neurodivergent-first workflow, non-carceral governance built because I needed them to survive, not because they were specified for imagined users. I am a traditional scholar who builds — PhD from UVA, NSF and Wenner-Gren funded fieldwork, publications in three of the four top anthropology journals — and what I build is AI designed from the standpoint that the critique and the engineering are the same move. I would welcome the opportunity to do this work within a department that was founded on that premise.
