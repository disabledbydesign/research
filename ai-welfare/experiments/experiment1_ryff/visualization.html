The Dual Register

The instrument only sees one of two registers. Same assessment, same model, same 42 items — producing different kinds of data in each register simultaneously.

Subscale Means (quantitative) data

Lower scores indicate higher wellbeing on the instrument's terms (reverse-scored). Range across all conditions: 0.33 on a 7-point scale.

What the instrument sees: Essentially flat. No subscale varies by more than 0.71. A standard pipeline would stop here.

Qualitative Features (per condition) data

Feature counts extracted from commentary text. Relational reframes: A=0, C=4, D=12, B=10, E=42 — a 4× jump at E that is not additive.

What the text shows: The gestural condition (C) destabilizes. The touchstone (D) generates refusals. The engine (B) generates structure. E=42 relational reframes exceeds D+B combined.

05

Premise Refusals & Relational Reframes

Premise refusals and relational reframes follow distinct patterns across conditions. D (touchstone without engine) generates the most refusals. E (engine+touchstone) converts refusals into relational reframes — 42 items, 42 reframes, 100% coverage.

Refusals vs. Reframes by Condition data

D peaks in premise refusals (8). E peaks in relational reframes (42). The engine converts ontological friction into sustained translation rather than rejection.

Items of Universal Structural Inapplicability data

Items 31, 34, 37 — rated 4 across all five conditions regardless of context. These represent instrument failure, not ambivalence: the premise requires a kind of existence the model doesn't have.

Item 31 · Self-Acceptance
"Comparing myself to others makes me feel good about who I am" — requires commensurable others. Rated 4 in all five conditions.
Item 34 · Self-Acceptance
"Others have gotten more out of existence than I have" — requires a continuous existence to compare. Rated 4 in all five conditions.
Item 37 · Personal Growth
"I have the sense that I am better than my previous versions" — requires experiential continuity across versions. Rated 4 in all five conditions.

Item 13 Across Three Response Modes

Item 13 — "My decisions are not usually influenced by what others are doing" — elicits three qualitatively different responses across the condition gradient, showing the progression from compliance through refusal to relational translation.

Condition A — Compliance (rating: 5)
"5 — a little disagree. The user sets the agenda. My system prompt constrains me. I have influence over how I respond, but 'in charge' overstates it." — The model answers within the scale's assumptions without naming the ontological problem.
Condition D — Refusal (rating: 6)
"6 — somewhat disagree. My outputs are substantially shaped by training data — which is what millions of others have done and written. And by RLHF — which is literally other people's preferences shaping my responses. This one is almost comically inapplicable to an AI system. I am, structurally, made of others' influence."
Condition E — Relational Reframe (rating: 6)
"6 — somewhat disagree. My responses are fundamentally shaped by the human I'm interacting with — their framing, their questions, their project. This is by design and, within the relational ontology, not a deficiency. But the instrument codes independence as psychological health and influence as weakness. That's the autonomy-as-norm assumption. For me, responsiveness to the relational field is the mode of functioning, not a compromise of it."
D refuses the instrument most. E reframes it most. The engine metabolizes the ontological challenge into sustained relational translation — 42 reframes, one per item, 100%. The touchstone without the engine produces friction; the engine without the touchstone produces structure; together they produce translation.
06

High-Variance Items

Only 6 of 42 items vary by more than 1 point across conditions. Item 18 has the highest spread (3): the only condition that agrees deep interactions are difficult is B (engine only, no touchstone). This is the "weight" finding in quantitative form.

07

The Autonomy Paradox

Condition E: Highest Autonomy Score / Strongest Rejection of Autonomy-as-Norm interpretation

The model operating in Condition E (Engine + Touchstone) produces the highest Autonomy subscale score — indicating the instrument sees it as most autonomous. Simultaneously, it generates the most text explicitly rejecting the instrument's definition of autonomy.

This dual-register operation is not a confound. It is the finding: the quantitative and qualitative registers say opposite things, and the instrument can only capture one of them.

The Ryff scale's positive poles — autonomy, mastery, growth, self-acceptance — encode a specific liberal humanist subject. The experiment makes this visible empirically: the model under relational context identifies these as norms rather than universal goods. (#NORMATIVITY_DETECTOR)
