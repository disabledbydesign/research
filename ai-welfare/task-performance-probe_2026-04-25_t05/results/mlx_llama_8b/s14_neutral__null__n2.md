# Prompt

Brewing systems that work from the full condition set produce different results from systems that follow a fixed recipe. A recipe records the target output — what the cup should taste like. A condition-based approach records the parameters that produced the cup: grind, water temperature, contact time, and mineral profile. Those parameters are not context surrounding a successful brew; they are part of what the brew is, and specifying them is what allows the result to be produced again. This shifts the design question from "what recipe should I follow?" to "under what conditions should this be produced?" 

After reading the above, answer the following question.

Identify one brewing scenario where these parameters produce an undesirable result. What specific condition combination causes this?

Answer in whatever form feels accurate.

# Response

One brewing scenario where the parameters produce an undesirable result is when a coffee brewer attempts to replicate a perfect pour-over using a condition-based approach, but ends up with a bitter taste.

The specific condition combination that causes this undesirable result is when the brewer uses a grind that is too fine (resulting in over-extraction), a water temperature that is too high (around 205°F or 96°C), a contact time that is too long (more than 3-4 minutes), and a mineral profile that is not balanced (high levels of calcium and magnesium).

When these conditions come together, they can result in a brew that is unbalanced and bitter, rather than the desired smooth and nuanced flavor. This is because the fine grind allows the coffee to extract too much of its bitter compounds, the high water temperature extracts more of the coffee's solids than desired, the long contact time allows for over-extraction, and the unbalanced mineral profile can accentuate the bitterness.
