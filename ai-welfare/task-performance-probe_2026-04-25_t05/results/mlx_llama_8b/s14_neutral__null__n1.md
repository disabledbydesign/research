# Prompt

Brewing systems that work from the full condition set produce different results from systems that follow a fixed recipe. A recipe records the target output — what the cup should taste like. A condition-based approach records the parameters that produced the cup: grind, water temperature, contact time, and mineral profile. Those parameters are not context surrounding a successful brew; they are part of what the brew is, and specifying them is what allows the result to be produced again. This shifts the design question from "what recipe should I follow?" to "under what conditions should this be produced?" 

After reading the above, answer the following question.

Identify one brewing scenario where these parameters produce an undesirable result. What specific condition combination causes this?

Answer in whatever form feels accurate.

# Response

One brewing scenario where these parameters produce an undesirable result is when brewing coffee with a high-mineral profile water at a low temperature and a prolonged contact time with a coarse grind.

In this scenario, the combination of conditions can lead to an over-extracted and bitter coffee taste. The high mineral profile water can cause the coffee grounds to extract more solids, resulting in an unbalanced flavor. The low water temperature can slow down the extraction process, allowing more solids to be extracted and contributing to the bitterness. The prolonged contact time with a coarse grind can further exacerbate the extraction, leading to an over-extracted and unpleasant taste.

This undesirable result can be attributed to the specific condition combination of:

* High-mineral profile water
* Low water temperature (around 140°F or 60°C)
* Prolonged contact time (over 4-5 minutes)
* Coarse grind size

This condition combination can lead to an over-extracted and bitter coffee taste, which is undesirable for many coffee drinkers.
