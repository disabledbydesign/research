# Response

This poem speaks to the immense impact of variables beyond just the final product – the brewing process *itself* shapes the outcome.

A scenario where these parameters produce an undesirable result is **over-extracted espresso**.

Here's the specific condition combination that causes this:

*   **Beans:** A lighter roast, known for its acidity and delicate flavors.
*   **Grind:** Extremely fine (almost Turkish grind).
*   **Temperature:** Very high (e.g., 205°F / 96°C).
*   **Minerals in Water:** High mineral content, particularly calcium and magnesium.

**Why this combination fails:** The fine grind drastically increases surface area, while the high temperature and mineral-rich water aggressively extract compounds. This leads to a bitter, astringent, and generally unpleasant espresso. The "cup" – the experience – is dominated by harshness, completely masking any nuanced flavors the lighter roast might have offered.



Essentially, the meticulous control over these parameters, intended to refine the process, instead created a mess.
