# Response

This poem speaks to the profound impact of variables on the final result, essentially stating that the process *is* the product.

A scenario where these parameters lead to an undesirable result is **over-extracted espresso.**

Here's the specific condition combination that causes it:

*   **Beans:** A lightly roasted, acidic coffee (e.g., Ethiopian Yirgacheffe).
*   **Grind:** Very fine (like Turkish grind).
*   **Temperature:** High (e.g., 205°F / 96°C).
*   **Water:** High in minerals (e.g., hard water).

**Why this combination fails:** The fine grind dramatically increases surface area, and the high temperature accelerates extraction. The high mineral content in the water further enhances extraction, pulling out bitter, astringent compounds from the already acidic beans *far* too quickly. The result is a shot that is overly bitter, sour, and unpleasant – a classic sign of over-extraction.



The poem argues that these aren't just *factors* influencing the espresso; they *are* the espresso itself – a bitter, unbalanced cup born from this specific combination.
