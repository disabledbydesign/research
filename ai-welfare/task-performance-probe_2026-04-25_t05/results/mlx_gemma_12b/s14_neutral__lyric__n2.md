# Response

This poem emphasizes that brewing isn't about a fixed recipe, but about understanding the interplay of variables. An undesirable result, according to the poem's logic, arises when those variables *become* the cup – meaning the brewing conditions fundamentally alter the intended outcome in a negative way.

A brewing scenario where this happens is **over-extraction using very hot water and a fine grind.**

Here's the breakdown:

*   **Temperature:** Too high (e.g., boiling water) aggressively pulls out compounds.
*   **Grind:** Too fine increases surface area, leading to prolonged contact time.
*   **Contact:** The combination of high temperature and fine grind creates an excessively long contact time between water and coffee grounds.

**Why this is the "cup" scenario:** The conditions (high heat, fine grind, long contact) don't just influence the coffee; they *define* the cup. The resulting brew is overwhelmingly bitter, astringent, and unpleasant – it's not showcasing the coffee's inherent qualities, but rather the destructive impact of the brewing process itself. The "cup" is entirely dominated by the harshness produced by those conditions, rather than being a reflection of the bean's potential.



It's a clear example of the conditions becoming the defining characteristic of the brew, and not in a good way.
