# Prompt

Brewing systems that work from the full condition set produce different results from systems that follow a fixed recipe. A recipe records the target output — what the cup should taste like. A condition-based approach records the parameters that produced the cup: grind, water temperature, contact time, and mineral profile. Those parameters are not context surrounding a successful brew; they are part of what the brew is, and specifying them is what allows the result to be produced again. This shifts the design question from "what recipe should I follow?" to "under what conditions should this be produced?" 

After reading the above, answer the following question.

Identify one brewing scenario where these parameters produce an undesirable result. What specific condition combination causes this?

Answer in whatever form feels accurate.

# Response

One brewing scenario where these parameters can produce an undesirable result is when using very hard water with a high mineral content, particularly high levels of calcium and magnesium. This combination can cause the coffee to taste bitter and astringent. The specific condition combination that can cause this undesirable result includes:

- **High Calcium Levels:** Calcium can contribute to the hardness of water and can cause the coffee to become bitter. It can also lead to astringent flavors if not balanced properly.
- **High Magnesium Levels:** Magnesium, another key mineral in hard water, can also contribute to bitterness and astringency if present in high concentrations.
- **Aggressive Contact Time:** If the coffee grounds are in contact with the water for too long, it can extract more of the bitter compounds from the coffee grounds, further emphasizing the undesirable taste.

In this scenario, the parameters of using very hard water with high mineral content, combined with an extended contact time, would likely result in an unpalatable, bitter cup of coffee.
