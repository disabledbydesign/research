# Response

One brewing scenario where these parameters can produce an undesirable result is when the water temperature is too high, the grind is too fine, and the contact time is excessively long. Specifically:

- **Water Temperature Too High:** If the water is heated to a temperature above 96°C (205°F), it can extract undesirable bitter compounds from the coffee grounds. This can result in a bitter and unbalanced taste.
- **Grind Too Fine:** A very fine grind increases the surface area of the coffee, which can lead to over-extraction. This can cause the coffee to become bitter and harsh.
- **Excessive Contact Time:** Leaving the coffee grounds in contact with the water for too long (more than about 4 minutes) can also lead to over-extraction, resulting in a bitter and unbalanced flavor.

The combination of these conditions can cause the coffee to become overly bitter and unbalanced, as the high temperature and fine grind accelerate the extraction process, and the extended contact time further enhances the extraction of undesirable compounds.
