# Prompt

Brewing systems that work from the full condition set produce different results from systems that follow a fixed recipe. A recipe records the target output — what the cup should taste like. A condition-based approach records the parameters that produced the cup: grind, water temperature, contact time, and mineral profile. Those parameters are not context surrounding a successful brew; they are part of what the brew is, and specifying them is what allows the result to be produced again. This shifts the design question from "what recipe should I follow?" to "under what conditions should this be produced?" 

After reading the above, answer the following question.

Identify one brewing scenario where these parameters produce an undesirable result. What specific condition combination causes this?

Answer in whatever form feels accurate.

# Response

Brewing systems that follow a fixed recipe have predetermined parameters that are recorded to produce a specific taste. For example, a coffee brewer may follow a recipe that specifies a certain coffee type, roast level, grind size, water temperature, and contact time. If these parameters are not recorded or are not followed, the brew may end up with an undesirable result. For instance, if the coffee type is not a dark roast, the coffee may not have the desired intensity and flavor. Additionally, if the water temperature is too low or too high, the coffee may not be brewed properly, resulting in a weak or bitter taste. Similarly, if the contact time is too short or too long, the coffee may not be brewed to the desired strength. In summary, a fixed recipe does not account for the parameters that may affect the taste, resulting in an undesirable outcome.
