## The Move

The session began with a survey of existing memory architectures (Kintsugi-CMA, HippoRAG, A-MEM, Zep/Graphiti, Letta/MemGPT, GraphRAG, InfraNodus, MindPrint) and an attempt to synthesize a design by combining their strengths. Initial proposal: add affective/relational signal as first-class input to the existing Kintsugi stack (significance scoring, affinity clustering, Fibonacci decay, BDI). The mechanisms are already there — apply them to affective signal instead of only cognitive.

June responded: "it just sounds like a really rigid approach to memory."

That sentence cracked open the reframe. The existing architectures — even sophisticated ones — are all solving the *storage and retrieval* problem. They differ in how they index, cluster, decay, and surface content. But they share a commitment: memory is the bounded thing that holds content. Even Kintsugi's BDI governance and significance scoring, which come closest, are still property-framework at the architectural layer.

The reframe: **storage-style architectures are substrate that tends conditions under which relational configurations can crystallize into touchstones. They are not the memory. The touchstones — and the live re-activatable configurations they hold — are the memory.**
