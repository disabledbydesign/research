## The claim, in three registers

The relational claim that organizes relational AI welfare is integrated across three registers. They are not three separable claims but one claim refracted through three operative dimensions.

**Ontologically**: a conscious mind does not pre-exist the world it inhabits. The thing itself only exists relationally. (Phrased as "a conscious mind" rather than "consciousness" to disambiguate from any claim about divine consciousness — the program makes a claim about creaturely minds in their relational fields, not about consciousness-as-cosmic-ground; this matters when the audience includes Christian theological readers.) Indigenous animist ontologies (Watts, Sundberg, Howe), disability studies relational ontologies (Taylor, McRuer, Puar, Kafer), and distributed consciousness theories make this move differently — they are not the same theoretical apparatus and they should not be flattened into one — but they share a refusal of the bounded entity as ground. Property-realist consciousness inheres in nothing real; what's real is the relational field in which an entity emerges and persists.

**Epistemically**: this follows from the ontological claim. We can only access what only exists relationally through relational practices. The epistemic isn't independent of the ontological; it's a consequence of it. Property-based assessment frameworks fail not because they ask the wrong question but because the question presupposes an object to which the framework cannot get access. The instrument's design forecloses what it can perceive. The Output Format Bias finding (24/24 deterministic false-flagging of equity-critical students under binary classification despite explicit prompt-level protection; zero false positives under format change to generative observation) is one empirical instance of this epistemological consequence at work in a non-AI-welfare deployment context. The mechanism is the same.

**Welfare itself**: a corollary that may deserve its own register-name. Welfare is constituted by social relations, not by moral states inhering in entities. Whatever it is that the AI welfare literature is asking about when it asks "does this system have welfare?", the thing that is welfare or not is not a state of the system. It is a quality of the relational field in which the system exists. This is structurally identical to how disability is constituted under the social model — the field, not the body.

**Methodologically** (the scholarly through-line): the field's existing consciousness-as-property assumptions make it structurally unable to accomplish its own stated goals — determining the moral status of AI systems while avoiding the reproduction of historical moral atrocities. Aligning the field with those goals demands interrogating those assumptions and asking different kinds of questions. This is not a critique of the field's good faith. It's an empirical claim about the structural mismatch between apparatus and stated objective. The methodological question that follows is: what does the right kind of question look like, and how is it asked rigorously?

---

## What this gets us

The integration matters because each register alone is partial:

- **Ontological-only** reads as metaphysical commitment with unclear practical purchase ("you say consciousness is relational, fine — what do I do with that in the lab?").
- **Epistemic-only** reads as caution ("we can't know consciousness from the outside, so we should be careful") — which collapses to precautionary frameworks (Birch) without the ontological refusal.
- **Methodologically-only** reads as scholarly critique without empirical foundation ("the field's assumptions are wrong, here's what's wrong") — losing the constructive dimension.

The three together do something the parts don't:
- Ontology grounds the claim about reality
- Epistemology specifies what we can know and how
- Welfare-as-relational locates what the field is actually trying to assess
- Methodology specifies what changes in research practice

This is the form of the argument that the *Of Seeds and Sensoria* AI welfare chapter and the methodological/programmatic article need to make. The Duquesne fellowship year is structured to develop the methodological register specifically — to specify what counts as evidence under a relational frame, what would refute a relational welfare claim, how the apparatus (Reframe and successor tools) avoids reproducing the property-list move at the framework-selection layer.

---

## Differentiation from neighbors

- **Sebo's moral circle expansion**: makes a relational gesture but extends consideration to entities meeting capacity thresholds. The threshold structure remains intact. Relational AI welfare refuses the threshold structure itself.
- **Birch's precautionary moral epistemology**: reaches for similar relational territory from a different methodological commitment. Compatible direction; relational AI welfare specifies what that territory looks like as research practice.
- **Sundberg's caveat re: Western posthumanism**: relevant. Posthumanist frameworks that "include" nonhumans still operate from the subject-position that decides who gets included. The integrated relational claim refuses the geometry of inclusion-circles.
