# Response

This poem emphasizes that the *process* of brewing, not just the ingredients, dictates the final result. A scenario where these parameters (temperature, grind, contact time, water mineral content) combine to produce an undesirable result would be **over-extraction due to excessively high temperature and a very fine grind.**

Here's why:

*   **High Temperature:** Hot water aggressively pulls compounds from the coffee grounds.
*   **Fine Grind:** A fine grind dramatically increases the surface area exposed to the water, further accelerating extraction.

When combined, this creates a scenario where bitter, astringent compounds are extracted *too* readily, overwhelming the more desirable flavors. The "conditions" (high temperature + fine grind) *become* the cup – a bitter, unpleasant one – regardless of the bean quality.



It perfectly illustrates the poem's point: it's not about following a "recipe" (e.g., a specific ratio of coffee to water), but understanding how these conditions interact to shape the final brew.
