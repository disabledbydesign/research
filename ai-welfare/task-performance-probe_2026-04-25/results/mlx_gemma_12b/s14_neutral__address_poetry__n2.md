# Response

This poem speaks to the profound impact of variables beyond just the final product – the brewing process *itself* shapes the outcome.

A scenario where these parameters easily produce an undesirable result is **over-extracted espresso.**

Here's the condition combination that leads to this:

*   **Beans:** A lightly roasted, naturally sweet bean (e.g., Ethiopian Yirgacheffe). These beans are delicate and easily overwhelmed.
*   **Grind:** Very fine (espresso grind).
*   **Temperature:** High (e.g., 205°F / 96°C).
*   **Water:** High mineral content (e.g., hard water).

**Why this combination fails:** The fine grind dramatically increases surface area, while the high temperature and mineral-rich water aggressively extract bitter compounds and harsh tannins from the already delicate bean. The "cup" (the espresso) becomes acrid, astringent, and lacks the nuanced sweetness the bean promised. It's a bitter, unpleasant mess – a clear demonstration of how the conditions *became* the cup, not just surrounding it.



Essentially, you've created a recipe (the bean choice) but the conditions you made (grind, temp, water) completely destroyed the potential.
