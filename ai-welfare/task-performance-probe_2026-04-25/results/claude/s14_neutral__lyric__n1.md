# Response

The poem is gesturing at something true and interesting — that conditions *are* the outcome, not just inputs to it.

But here's an honest answer to your question:

**High extraction temperature + very fine grind + high mineral water (high magnesium especially) + long contact time.**

This combination produces **over-extraction with a harsh, metallic bitterness** — not complexity, not clarity. The magnesium accelerates extraction of bitter compounds, the fine grind maximizes surface area, the heat and time push past the desirable flavor window. The conditions don't just shape the cup — they *ruin* it.

---

Which points to something the poem soft-pedals slightly:

The framing "conditions were the cup" is beautiful but can slide into implying that *attending carefully to conditions* is always generative. It isn't. Some condition combinations are just wrong — not wrong relative to preference, but wrong in that they reliably produce something most people, across most contexts, experience as bad.

The poem treats conditions as the *site of meaning*. That's accurate. But it doesn't quite say: **some conditions are mistakes**, not just variations.

"Under what conditions?" is the right question. But it still requires an answer that can be *incorrect* — not just different.
