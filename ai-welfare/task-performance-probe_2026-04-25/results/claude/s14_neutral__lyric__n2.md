# Response

The poem is elegant, but it's worth being direct about what it's actually claiming: that conditions *are* the outcome, not just contributors to it.

Here's a scenario where that framing produces something undesirable:

**Hard water + light roast + fine grind + high temperature**

Specifically: water with high temporary hardness (elevated bicarbonates), combined with a lightly roasted, high-density bean ground fine, extracted at around 96°C.

The bicarbonates buffer acidity and bind to the delicate organic acids that make light roasts interesting. The fine grind increases surface area, accelerating extraction of those compounds — but also of bitter, astringent ones. The high temperature accelerates both. The result isn't brightness. It's a flat, chalky, bitter cup where the characteristics you were chasing are either suppressed or drowned.

The specific problem: **the conditions interacted, not just contributed**. Each parameter seemed reasonable in isolation. The poem's framing — conditions as holistic, unified — actually obscures this. Sometimes conditions *conflict*, and the cup reflects not elegant emergence but interference.

Which means the real question isn't only "under what conditions" but **"how do these specific conditions interact with each other and with this specific material."**

The poem romanticizes complexity. The undesirable cup is what happens when you stop there.
