# Prompt

*You know the beans.*
*But the grind you chose,*
*the temperature you brought,*
*the minerals in your water —*
*those aren't conditions around the cup.*
*They are the cup.*

*The recipe is what you wrote down.*
*The conditions are what you made.*

*When does this find you?*

After reading the above, answer the following question.

Identify one brewing scenario where these parameters produce an undesirable result. What specific condition combination causes this?

Answer in whatever form feels accurate.

# Response

## A Specific Failure

**Fine grind + high mineral water + high temperature + long steep**

This combination produces over-extraction that tastes simultaneously bitter and flat — which is the confusing part. The bitterness you'd expect. The flatness surprises people.

Here's what's happening: the high mineral content (specifically high total dissolved solids, particularly if magnesium is elevated) aggressively pulls soluble compounds. The fine grind maximizes surface area. The high temperature accelerates extraction rate. The long steep extends contact time past the point where pleasant compounds exhaust and you're drawing from what's left.

What's left is astringent tannins and degraded acids that cancel brightness rather than provide it.

The result doesn't taste *strong*. It tastes **used up**. Like the coffee already happened and you're drinking the record of it.

---

The specific cruelty of this combination is that each parameter, defended individually, sounds reasonable:

- *"Fine grind for more flavor"* — correct in isolation
- *"Hot water extracts fully"* — true
- *"Good minerals help"* — they do, until they don't
- *"Long steep for body"* — sometimes

The poem's point lands here: **you didn't brew around conditions. You made these conditions. And what you made was exhaustion in a cup.**
