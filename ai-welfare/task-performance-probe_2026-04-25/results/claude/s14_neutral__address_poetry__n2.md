# Response

## A Scenario Where the Parameters Betray You

**Fine grind + high mineral water + high temperature + long steep.**

Each one seems like a commitment to quality. Together they produce something harsh and medicinal — over-extracted, with the dissolved minerals (particularly calcium and magnesium at elevated levels) amplifying bitter compounds that a softer water would have left behind.

The specific mechanism: fine grind increases surface area dramatically. High temperature accelerates extraction rate. Long steep extends contact time. Hard water doesn't just carry flavor — it **pulls** certain compounds more aggressively, particularly chlorogenic acids and caffeine byproducts.

The result isn't strong coffee. It's **exhausted** coffee. Everything extractable has been extracted, including what should have stayed in the grounds.

---

The poem's framing is honest here too: each of those choices felt intentional. Fine grind — precision. Good mineral water — care. High heat — confidence. Full steep — patience.

But intention doesn't override chemistry. The conditions you made can still be the wrong ones.

**What causes this specifically:** the combination eliminates every natural limiting factor that would have stopped extraction at the right moment. Nothing was left to act as a brake.

That's the scenario. You made it thoroughly, and it's thoroughly undrinkable.
