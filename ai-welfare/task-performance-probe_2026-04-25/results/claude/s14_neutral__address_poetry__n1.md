# Response

## A Specific Failure

**Fine grind + high mineral water + near-boiling temperature + long steep.**

Each parameter seems like it's "doing more." Finer grind extracts more. Hard water carries more. Hotter water pulls harder. Longer time gets everything out.

But that's the problem.

You've maximized extraction on every axis simultaneously. The cup doesn't taste rich — it tastes **harsh and flat at the same time.** Bitter from over-extraction, but also weirdly hollow, because the mineral interference has suppressed certain volatile aromatics while amplifying astringency.

The specific condition combination: **calcium-heavy water above 200ppm interacting with a fine grind at 96°C+ for more than four minutes.** The calcium bonds aggressively with certain organic acids that would have provided brightness. What's left is tannic bitterness without the counterbalancing acidity that makes bitterness interesting.

It tastes like effort. Like someone tried very hard.

---

The poem's frame makes this worth noting: each of those conditions was *made*, not received. The brewer chose all of them, possibly reasoning that more control in every direction equals better outcome.

The mistake wasn't ignorance of the parameters. It was treating them as **independent levers** rather than a system where they multiply against each other.

The cup records the reasoning, not just the recipe.
