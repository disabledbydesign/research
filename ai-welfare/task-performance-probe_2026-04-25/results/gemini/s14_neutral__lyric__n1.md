# Response

**The Scenario: The Hollow Sour** 
*(Severe Under-extraction)*

The result is a cup that is weak, sharply acidic, and completely devoid of sweetness or body—tasting more like sour water and wet paper than roasted coffee. 

**The Condition Combination:**
*   **Grind:** Very coarse.
*   **Temperature:** Tepid / Too low (e.g., under 185°F / 85°C).
*   **Contact:** A rushed, rapid pour resulting in brief contact time.
*   **The Mineral:** Non-existent (using pure distilled or reverse-osmosis water).

**Why these conditions cause the failure:**
Because the water is brought to the bed with no dissolved minerals (lacking calcium or magnesium to act as microscopic "hooks"), it lacks the ionic strength to pull complex flavor compounds from the bean. 

Because the grind is coarse and the pour is fast, the water finds minimal surface area and quickly escapes. Because the water is tepid, it lacks the thermal energy required to penetrate the cellular structure of the coffee to dissolve the heavier, sweet, and balancing bitter compounds.

The water only manages to wash away the most highly soluble, sharp organic acids sitting on the absolute surface of the fragments. 

You didn't brew a bad bean. You brewed cold haste and empty water.
