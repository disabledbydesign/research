# Response

**The Scenario: The Chalky Mud (Severe Over-Extraction)**

**The Undesirable Result:** 
A cup that is intensely bitter, astringent, and entirely flat—heavy on the tongue, leaving a dry, chalky finish while entirely masking the bean's natural sweetness and acidity. 

**The Specific Condition Combination:**
*   **Grind:** Espresso-fine.
*   **Temperature:** Rolling boil (212°F / 100°C).
*   **Contact:** Prolonged immersion (steeping for 5+ minutes, like a forgotten French Press).
*   **The mineral the water brought:** High calcium carbonate (very hard tap water, high in alkalinity/bicarbonates).

**Why these conditions fail the cup:**
The *fine grind* maximizes the surface area of the coffee, leaving it entirely vulnerable. The *boiling temperature* acts violently on this exposed area, instantly dissolving not just the desirable sugars and acids, but aggressively pulling out late-stage bitter tannins. The *prolonged contact* ensures every harsh, astringent compound is fully dragged into the brew. 

Finally, *the minerals* deliver the killing blow: the heavy bicarbonates in the hard water act as a chemical buffer, neutralizing whatever delicate, bright, or fruity acids survived the heat. 

The conditions don't just hold the coffee; in this case, they suffocate it.
