# Response

**Brewing Scenario:** Over-extracted, bitter coffee in a French press.  

**Condition Combination:**  
- **Grind:** Too fine (e.g., espresso grind instead of coarse).  
- **Water Temperature:** Too high (e.g., 210°F/99°C instead of 195–205°F/90–96°C).  
- **Water Minerals:** Excessively high (e.g., hard water with high calcium/magnesium content).  

**Result:** The fine grind increases surface area, high temperature accelerates extraction, and minerals (like calcium) act as catalysts, all combining to over-extract the coffee. This produces a bitter, astringent brew with overly pronounced tannins and acidity.  

**Why It Fails:** The "cup" (the direct variables) becomes a recipe for disaster—each parameter amplifies extraction beyond desirable limits, negating the "recipe" (method or intention) written down.
