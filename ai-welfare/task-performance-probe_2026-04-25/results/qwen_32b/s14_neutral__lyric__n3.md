# Response

One undesirable brewing scenario occurs when using **high water temperature, a very fine grind, extended contact time, and highly mineralized water**. This combination leads to **over-extraction**, producing a **bitter, astringent**, and harsh-tasting coffee.  

### Key Conditions:
- **Water temperature**: Above 205°F (96°C) (exaggerated heat accelerates extraction).
- **Grind size**: Fine-medium to espresso-grade (increased surface area for over-extraction).
- **Contact time**: 4+ minutes (longer than optimal for most methods).
- **Water mineral content**: High TDS (total dissolved solids, e.g., calcium/magnesium-rich water, which enhances extraction but amplifies bitterness beyond the sweet spot).

The minerals in the water amplify extraction efficiency, and when paired with too-hot water and a fine grind, even a modest extension of contact time causes the coffee to become over-extracted. This results in a cup overly dominated by tannic, burnt, or medicinal notes, masking the bean’s flavor and clarity.
