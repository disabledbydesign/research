# Response

**Brewing Scenario:**  
Over-extracted French Press coffee due to a **very fine grind, high water temperature (205°F), and water high in mineral content (e.g., 300+ ppm TDS)**.  

**Explanation:**  
- **Fine grind**: Increases surface area, accelerating water flow through the grounds.  
- **High temperature (205°F)**: Enhances compound dissolution, speeding up extraction.  
- **High mineral content water**: Minerals like calcium/magnesium boost extraction efficiency.  

**Combined Effect**:  
This trifecta creates **over-extraction**, where the water pulls excessive chlorogenic acids and polyphenols from the coffee. The result is a **bitter, astringent, muddy cup** with undesirable metallic or sour notes post-bitterness. The minerals amplify extraction, while the fine grind and high heat prevent proper diffusion, leaving the coffee clogged and unbalanced.  

**Key Lesson**:  
Even in methods like French Press, which tolerate coarser grinds, pushing boundaries in all three parameters simultaneously disrupts the brewing equilibrium, undermining the "conditions *are* the cup" philosophy of controlled, harmonic extraction.
