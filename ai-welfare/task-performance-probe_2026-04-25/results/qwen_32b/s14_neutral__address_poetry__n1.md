# Response

**Undesirable Brewing Scenario: Over-extraction due to Fine Grind, High Temperature, and Mineral-Rich Water**  

**Condition Combination**:  
- **Grind Size**: Very fine (espresso-like) for a pour-over or drip method (which typically uses medium-coarse).  
- **Water Temperature**: Too high (205°F+), accelerating extraction.  
- **Water Minerals**: High mineral content (e.g., water with excessive calcium or magnesium hardness).  

**Why This Fails**:  
The fine grind increases surface area, the high temperature speeds up extraction, and mineral-rich water enhances extraction efficiency. Combined, they cause **over-extraction**, resulting in **bitter, astringent coffee**. The parameters synergistically over-extract the coffee’s tannins and bitter compounds, making the cup unbalanced and unpleasant.  

This scenario arises when someone clings to a written "recipe" (e.g., using espresso grind size and water temperatures) for a brewing method (like pour-over) without adapting to the tool’s natural dynamics—ignoring that *the conditions you make* (grind, temp, water) define the cup itself.
