# Response

One brewing scenario where these parameters produce an undesirable result is when the water temperature is too high, the grind is too fine, and the contact time is too long, resulting in an over-extracted and bitter cup.

Specifically, if the temperature of the water is around 205°F (96°C), the grind is extremely fine (almost powder-like), and the coffee grounds are in contact with the water for 5-6 minutes, the resulting cup will likely be unpalatable. The high temperature will extract too much from the beans, the fine grind will allow too much of the coffee's solids to dissolve, and the prolonged contact time will amplify these effects, leading to a bitter and astringent taste.

In this case, the "mineral the water brought with it" could also play a role, as high levels of certain minerals like calcium or magnesium can further accentuate the bitterness and unpleasant flavors. 

This combination of conditions would indeed produce a very different (and undesirable) cup, even with the same high-quality beans.
