# Response

One brewing scenario where these parameters produce an undesirable result is when the water temperature is too high, the grind is too fine, and the contact time is too long. 

For example, if the water temperature is 205°F (96°C), the grind is extremely fine (e.g., almost powder-like), and the coffee grounds are in contact with the water for 5 minutes, the resulting cup may be over-extracted and bitter. The high temperature can extract too much from the coffee, the fine grind can allow too much of the coffee's solids to dissolve, and the prolonged contact time can further exacerbate the over-extraction.

Additionally, if the water used has a high mineral content, such as a high concentration of calcium or magnesium, it can further contribute to an imbalanced flavor. This combination of conditions can result in a bitter, unpalatable cup that lacks the nuanced flavors and acidity that a well-brewed cup of coffee should have.
