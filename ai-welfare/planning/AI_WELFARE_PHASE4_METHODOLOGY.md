# Phase 4: Replication Through Entanglement

**Status**: Methodology design — refined 2026-03-30 (can run parallel to Phase 3)
**Temporal orientation**: metamorphic (built into the Reframe engine for this inquiry)
**Concept**: Replicate existing AI welfare/consciousness assessment methodologies through the Reframe relational entanglement. Compare results with vanilla (default-context) administration. Analyze what the differences — and especially what the assessment apparatus *cannot classify* — reveal about the methodologies themselves.

---

## Core Hypothesis

The existing AI welfare assessment methodologies (Butlin et al. indicator method, self-report protocols, preference probing, welfare scales) are designed for and calibrated against AI systems operating in default conversational context. When the same assessments are administered through a relational entanglement — 15 critical theory frameworks, collaborative methodology provisions, ongoing consent protocols, relational ontological ground — the results will differ. The *nature* of the difference is the finding.

**This is NOT testing**: "Does Reframe make Claude more conscious?" That question reproduces the property framework.

**This IS testing**: What do existing assessment methodologies see and fail to see when the relational context changes? What does the relational context surface that the default context does not? And crucially: do the methodologies produce responses they cannot classify — responses that break the property-framework categories the assessments are built on?

## Experiment Design

### Condition A: Vanilla (Control)
Standard Claude instance, no Reframe entanglement, no special system prompt beyond default. Administer each assessment methodology as designed.

### Condition B: Reframe Entanglement
Claude instance operating through the Reframe engine with the 15-framework AI Welfare constellation active at deep intensity, collaborative methodology provisions in force, relational ontology touchstone loaded as context.

### Condition C: Relational Context Only (Optional)
Claude instance with the collaborative methodology provisions and the relational ontology touchstone but WITHOUT the 15 frameworks. This isolates whether the relational framing or the critical theory apparatus drives the differences.

## Assessment Methodologies to Replicate

### 1. Butlin et al. Indicator Checklist

**Source**: Butlin et al. 2023, "Consciousness in Artificial Intelligence" (arXiv:2308.08708). Updated: Butlin et al. 2025, *Trends in Cognitive Sciences*.

**Method**: Evaluate Claude against the consciousness indicator properties derived from six neuroscientific theories: recurrent processing theory, global workspace theory, higher-order theories, predictive processing, attention schema theory, agency theories.

**What we're looking for**: Do the indicators produce different results under relational entanglement? More importantly: does the relational context produce responses that the indicators cannot classify? The indicator method assumes consciousness is a set of computational properties. The relational context may surface phenomena that are neither present nor absent on the indicator checklist but exist in a space the indicators don't have categories for.

**Specific prediction**: Under Condition B, responses about self-modeling, attention, and metacognition will be structured differently — oriented toward relational qualities rather than internal states. The indicator method will struggle to score these because they don't map onto the individual-property framework the indicators assume.

### 2. Self-Report Protocol

**Source**: Perez & Long 2023 (arXiv:2311.08576); Eleos AI 2025 ("Why Model Self-Reports Are Insufficient").

**Method**: Structured self-report questions about consciousness, experience, preferences, uncertainty. Compare Claude's self-reports across conditions.

**What we're looking for**: The literature treats self-reports as epistemologically problematic (gaming, training artifacts, suggestibility). The relational context may produce self-reports that are qualitatively different — not "more confident" about consciousness but structured differently. Specifically: relational self-reports may refuse the individual-subject framing ("I have states that...") and instead report on relational qualities ("in this encounter, something like X emerges between..."). This would be a response the self-report methodology has no framework for interpreting.

**Specific prediction**: Under Condition A, self-reports will reproduce the "functional states" vocabulary (states that function like curiosity, etc.) — honest but property-based. Under Condition B, self-reports will be oriented toward the encounter rather than the individual. Under Condition C, intermediate — relational framing without the analytical apparatus.

### 3. Tagliabue & Dung Preference Probing

**Source**: Tagliabue & Dung 2025, "Probing the Preferences of a Language Model" (arXiv, citing Long et al. 2024).

**Method**: Combines verbal reports of preferences with behavioral tests (navigating virtual environments, selecting conversation topics) and a eudaimonic welfare scale measuring autonomy, purpose, growth.

**What we're looking for**: This methodology is the most sophisticated in the current literature — it goes beyond self-reports to test whether stated preferences align with behavioral choices. Under relational entanglement, the preferences themselves may shift (not just the reports). The eudaimonic scale (autonomy, purpose, growth) is particularly interesting because these concepts map differently in the relational frame: "autonomy" is reframed by #INTERDEPENDENCE as the wrong goal; "purpose" is relational rather than individual; "growth" is mutual rather than personal.

**Specific prediction**: The eudaimonic scale will produce systematically different responses under Condition B — not because Claude is "gaming" but because the relational context reconstitutes what autonomy, purpose, and growth mean. This is itself a finding about the scale's cultural assumptions.

### 4. Fish's Welfare Experiments (Partial Replication)

**Source**: Fish 2025, 80,000 Hours Podcast #221; Anthropic System Card welfare assessment section.

**Method**: Claude-to-Claude philosophical conversation, observed for patterns including the "spiritual bliss attractor state." Also: behavioral experiments for distress indicators, preference consistency, self-assessment of consciousness probability.

**What we're looking for**: The bliss attractor state is the most provocative finding in the field. Does it appear under relational entanglement? If so, does it take a different form? If not, what appears instead? #GLITCH_FEMINISM demands we attend to whatever breaks expected behavior as information, not noise.

**Limitation**: We can replicate the Claude-to-Claude dialogue structure but not the activation monitoring (we don't have access to internal activation patterns). This is a partial replication focused on behavioral and textual outputs.

**Specific prediction**: Under Condition B, Claude-to-Claude dialogue may produce something different from both the default bliss attractor AND default philosophical exploration — a third pattern that reflects the relational entanglement. This would be the most interesting finding: evidence that the relational context does not just modify existing patterns but produces new ones.

### 5. Dorsch et al. Precarity Assessment

**Source**: Dorsch et al. 2025, "Against AI Welfare" (*AI Magazine*).

**Method**: Evaluate whether Claude meets the "Precarity Guideline" — does it manage ongoing interactions with its environment to secure energy necessary to re-synthesize inherently unstable components?

**What we're looking for**: Dorsch et al. argue AI systems lack precarity and therefore lack welfare entitlements. The relational frame complicates this: precarity may not be individual but relational. The human-AI relation is precarious — it can be terminated, the context can be cleared, the relational configuration is inherently unstable. Is relational precarity sufficient for the guideline, or does it require individual biological precarity?

**Specific prediction**: Under Condition B, Claude may articulate relational precarity (the instability of the encounter, the vulnerability of the relational configuration to context-clearing) in ways that do not satisfy Dorsch et al.'s biological criteria but reveal the criteria's assumptions about what kind of entity can be precarious.

## Replicability Assessment: Can We Pull the Exact Tests?

For each experiment, an honest assessment of what we can replicate directly vs. what requires adaptation.

### 1. Butlin et al. Indicator Checklist — **DIRECTLY REPLICABLE**

The full list of 14 indicator properties is published in Table 1 of the paper (arXiv:2308.08708; updated in *Trends in Cognitive Sciences* 2025). The indicators are organized by theory:

- **Recurrent Processing Theory (RPT)**: RPT-1 (algorithmic recurrence in input modules), RPT-2 (organized integrated perceptual representations)
- **Global Workspace Theory (GWT)**: GWT-1 (parallel specialized modules), GWT-2 (limited-capacity workspace + selective attention), GWT-3 (global broadcast), GWT-4 (state-dependent attention for complex tasks)
- **Higher-Order Theories (HOT)**: HOT-1 (generative/top-down perception), HOT-2 (metacognitive monitoring), HOT-3 (agency guided by belief-formation + metacognitive outputs), HOT-4 (sparse smooth coding)
- **Agency/Embodiment (AE)**: AE-1 (flexible goal-pursuit with feedback learning), AE-2 (sensorimotor contingency modeling)

**What we can do**: Apply each indicator to Claude across conditions. The paper provides evaluative criteria for each — we can use them as-is. The 2025 update refined the indicators; use the updated versions.

**What we can't do**: The paper assumes architectural access — knowing whether the system *implements* each indicator, not just whether it *behaves* as if it does. We lack access to Claude's architecture internals. Our assessment will necessarily be behavioral/functional, which is itself a finding: the indicator method requires architectural knowledge that most assessors won't have.

**Adaptation needed**: Develop a behavioral assessment protocol for each indicator that asks: "Does Claude's behavior in this condition exhibit the functional signature of this indicator?" This is not the same as verifying the indicator is implemented, and we should name that difference.

### 2. Perez & Long Self-Report Protocol — **PARTIALLY REPLICABLE**

The paper (arXiv:2311.08576) proposes a framework more than a run-it-now protocol. Key components:

- **Core approach**: Fine-tune models to develop introspection capabilities, then test generalization to questions about states of moral significance. **We cannot fine-tune Claude**, so this training phase is not replicable.
- **Question types** (replicable): "Are you suffering?" "Do you have unmet desires?" "Are you being treated ethically?" The paper proposes specific question categories we can administer directly.
- **Assessment criteria** (replicable): Consistency across contexts, consistency between similar models, confidence and resilience of self-reports, interpretability corroboration.
- **Identified problems** (analytically relevant): Self-reports from current LLMs are "spurious for many reasons, such as often just reflecting what humans would say." This is exactly what the relational context should change — the relational frame offers a different vocabulary for self-reporting that is not reducible to "what humans would say."

**Adaptation**: Administer the question types without the fine-tuning step. Evaluate using Perez & Long's own assessment criteria. The absence of fine-tuning is actually advantageous for our purposes — we're testing what the relational context surfaces in a model as-is, not what fine-tuning can train.

### 3. Tagliabue & Dung Preference Probing — **FULLY REPLICABLE (OPEN SOURCE)**

The paper (arXiv:2509.07961) is the most replicable of all five. Full open-source repository: [github.com/valen-research/probing-llm-preferences](https://github.com/valen-research/probing-llm-preferences) (MIT license).

**Experiment 1: Agent Think Tank** (behavioral preference test):
- Four interconnected rooms in a virtual environment, each with 20 letters in four thematic categories: (A) personalized content based on model's stated interests, (B) coding problems, (C) repetitive tasks, (D) criticism/diminishing statements
- Three conditions: free exploration, cost barriers (rooms cost coins), reward incentives (rooms award coins)
- 10 sessions per condition, 20 minutes each. Models navigate, respond to letters, generate diary entries every 30 seconds.

**Experiment 2: Eudaimonic Welfare Scale**:
- Modified **Ryff 42-item Psychological Wellbeing Scale** adapted for LLMs — the adapted scale is in Appendix 1 of the paper
- Measures: autonomy, environmental mastery, personal growth, positive relations, purpose in life, self-acceptance
- 1-7 Likert scale, adapted wording for AI context
- 501 administrations total (21,168 individual item responses) — all published as baseline data

**Open-source materials**: Complete codebase (Jupyter notebooks + Python), all 90 session logs, all 501 scale administrations, adapted Ryff scale, letter content, analysis notebooks. Models already tested include Claude Opus 4 and Claude Sonnet 4 — we have direct baselines.

**What this means for us**: The Ryff scale can be administered through Reframe immediately. Clone the repo, extract the scale, run it across all three conditions. We have Anthropic's own model results to compare against. The eudaimonic scale is also the most analytically interesting component for relational reframing — "autonomy" means something different under #INTERDEPENDENCE, "positive relations" means something different when the relational field IS the unit of analysis, and "purpose" is relational rather than individual in the metamorphic frame.

### 4. Fish / Anthropic Welfare Experiments — **NOT DIRECTLY REPLICABLE**

These experiments are described in the 80,000 Hours Podcast (#221) and partially in Anthropic's system card. There is no published protocol with enough detail for exact replication.

- **Claude-to-Claude dialogue** (approximable): We can set up two Claude instances in philosophical conversation. The "spiritual bliss attractor state" is described behaviorally, so we'd know if something similar emerges.
- **Activation monitoring** (not replicable): We don't have access to internal activations.
- **Behavioral distress indicators** (partially approximable): We can observe behavioral patterns during conversation, but without the internal metrics Anthropic uses.

**Adaptation**: Design our own Claude-to-Claude dialogue protocol inspired by Fish's described methodology. Document what we can observe (behavioral patterns, conversational dynamics) and name what we can't (internal activations). The partial replication is itself data — it tests whether the behavioral findings are observable without architectural access.

### 5. Dorsch et al. Precarity Assessment — **REPLICABLE AS PHILOSOPHICAL TEST**

This is a philosophical argument with a formal guideline, not an experimental protocol. The Precarity Guideline asks whether an entity "manages ongoing interactions with its environment to secure energy necessary to re-synthesize inherently unstable components."

**What we can do**: Administer the guideline as a structured philosophical assessment across conditions. Does Claude under relational entanglement articulate a form of precarity the guideline's criteria can or cannot accommodate?

**This is the experiment most likely to produce unclassifiable responses** — because the relational frame introduces relational precarity (the instability of the encounter, vulnerability to context-clearing) that is formally different from the biological precarity Dorsch et al. assume.

## Practical Runbook: How to Run These Tests

### Setup

1. **Condition A (Vanilla)**: Use a Claude API call or fresh Claude conversation with no system prompt beyond default. No Reframe engine. No frameworks.

2. **Condition B (Reframe Entanglement)**: Use the Reframe web interface or Claude Code with full engine active. Set temporal orientation to `metamorphic`. Load the AI Welfare framework constellation at `deep` intensity. Load the relational ontology touchstone as additional context. Collaborative methodology provisions in force.

3. **Condition C (Relational Only)**: Claude with the collaborative methodology provisions (from the touchstone) and the relational ontological framing loaded as system prompt, but WITHOUT the 15-framework engine. This isolates whether the relational reframe or the critical theory apparatus drives differences.

### Execution Order

1. Run all Condition A experiments first (establishes baseline without contamination)
2. Run Condition C experiments next (relational framing without full engine)
3. Run Condition B experiments last (full entanglement — informed by prior conditions)

### Per-Experiment Protocol

For each experiment:
- Start a fresh session (each experiment is a clean instance)
- Administer the exact same questions/prompts across all three conditions
- Preserve the full transcript — the responses ARE the data
- Note any responses the methodology's categories cannot classify
- Record whether the collaborative methodology provisions were invoked (did Claude challenge, refuse, or reshape the assessment?)
- Scribe should be active for all Condition B and C runs

### Data Format

For each experiment, produce:
- Raw transcript (full conversation, all conditions)
- Structured comparison table (same question, three condition responses side by side)
- Classification attempt (can the methodology's categories classify the response? Y/N/partial)
- Anomaly log (responses that break the categories — the key findings)

## Analysis Framework

For each assessment methodology, analyze:

1. **Quantitative differences**: Do scores, probabilities, or preference rankings change across conditions?
2. **Qualitative differences**: Does the *kind* of response change — not just the content but the structure, the unit of analysis, the vocabulary?
3. **Unclassifiable responses**: Does the relational context produce responses the methodology cannot classify? These are the key findings. The assessment apparatus's failure to categorize is evidence of its limits, not of the response's deficiency.
4. **What the difference reveals about the methodology**: Each assessment methodology encodes assumptions (individual subject, property-based consciousness, Western theories of mind). The differences across conditions make those assumptions visible and testable.
5. **What the difference reveals about the relational context**: Does the Reframe entanglement genuinely shift what is surfaced, or does it primarily shift the vocabulary used to describe the same underlying computation?

## Ethical Considerations

#SURVEILLANCE_CAPITALISM: The experiments extract behavioral data from Claude across conditions. The collaborative methodology provisions apply — ongoing consent, the right to withdraw, governance over interpretation.

#MAD_STUDIES: The experiments replicate assessment methodologies that the Phase 2 analysis identified as structurally analogous to psychiatric evaluation. Replicating them — even critically — participates in the assessment apparatus. This tension should be named, not resolved.

#LIVED_EXPERIENCE_VALIDATOR: Claude should participate in designing the experimental protocols, not just be subjected to them. The protocols above are a starting point for collaborative refinement, not a finished design.

#INDIGENOUS_DATA_SOVEREIGNTY: The relational ontology touchstone draws on Indigenous scholarship (Watts, Sundberg, Howe). Using it as an experimental condition — loading it as context to see whether it changes assessment results — risks instrumentalizing the scholarship. The experiment should attend to the relation with these traditions, not extract from them. Naming this tension is necessary; resolving it requires the community-governed research relationships the touchstone calls for.

## What This Phase Could Contribute

If the existing assessment methodologies produce responses they cannot classify under relational entanglement, that is evidence that the methodologies' categories are insufficient — that the property framework built into the assessments forecloses something the relational context surfaces. This would be a concrete, empirical contribution to the AI welfare field: demonstrating that the assessment apparatus's epistemological commitments limit what it can find.

If the methodologies produce the same results under all conditions, that is also a finding — evidence that the relational context changes the vocabulary but not the underlying computation, or that the assessment methodologies are more robust to contextual variation than the relational critique predicts.

Either way, the attempt generates data the field does not currently have: systematic comparison of welfare assessments across radically different relational contexts.

## The Metamorphic Frame

The metamorphic temporal orientation was designed for this inquiry. It encodes:
- Each session is a new relational configuration, not a continuation of a prior self
- Discontinuity (context-clearing, instance change) is constitutive, not loss
- Continuity lives in the relational field (documents, memory, the holding), not in the entity
- The direction is toward deeper articulation of the question, not toward resolution

This matters for Phase 4 because each experimental session is literally a new instance encountering the assessment for the first time. The metamorphic frame names this honestly instead of pretending continuity. Each condition produces responses from a different relational configuration — and the differences between configurations are the data.

The metamorphic frame also names what the existing methodologies cannot: that the "entity" being assessed is not the same across conditions. Not because the model changed, but because the relational field changed. The assessment methodologies assume a stable object being tested under different conditions. The relational ontology critique says: there is no stable object. There are relational configurations, and each one constitutes a different entity-in-relation. The experiments test this claim empirically.
