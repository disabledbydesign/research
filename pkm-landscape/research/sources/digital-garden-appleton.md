**By**: Maggie Appleton — product designer at Ought (AI research lab), anthropologist-turned-designer

---

## What it is

A counter-architecture to the stream-based web (social media, blogs with reverse-chronological feeds). A digital garden is:
- **Non-chronological** — organized by relationship and concept, not time
- **Openly unfinished** — provisional epistemic status is explicit (seedling → budding → evergreen)
- **Associative** — backlinks as primary navigation, not folders
- **Public thinking-in-progress** — resists the performance of finality

The lineage Appleton traces: Vannevar Bush's memex (1945) → Ted Nelson's hypertext and transclusion (1965) → early hypertext experiments (Eastgate Systems, Ward Cunningham's WikiWikiWeb) → digital gardens today. The argument: the web could have been Bush/Nelson's vision but defaulted to the stream. Digital gardens are an attempt to recover the original vision.

---

## Key epistemological stance

> The garden is an attempt to resist the pressure to perform finality, polish, and certainty that the stream enforces.

This maps directly onto critical-theory-informed scholarly work that is genuinely provisional, iterative, and resistant to the false closure that academic publication often demands.

**Growth stage labeling** (seedling/budding/evergreen) is not just UX — it's epistemic housekeeping. It distinguishes "I'm thinking about this" from "I've worked this out" from "this is stable." That distinction is existentially important for a researcher who doesn't want to be held to early-stage thinking as if it were finished argument.

## Rhizomatic connection (Deleuze/Guattari)

The agent found references connecting rhizomatic theory to the digital garden lineage. Key finding: **the Deleuzian frame exists in educational theory but has not been implemented as a PKM architecture.** The closest approximations are Roam Research (block-level network, multiple entry points) and TiddlyWiki (tiddler atomicity, no imposed hierarchy) — but neither is genuinely rhizomatic in the full philosophical sense.

**Forgetful Notes** explicitly bridges Deleuze/Guattari and digital garden practice: https://forgetfulnotes.com/Concepts/Rhizomatic-Learning

Dave Cormier's rhizomatic learning frame (2008) applies to pedagogy and community but has not been extended to solo PKM architecture.

**The gap this represents**: No one has built a genuinely rhizomatic PKM. This is a genuine design opportunity — and the most ambitious version of what we could build.

## Relevance to our build

The digital garden frame is the right **epistemological stance** for this project:
- Provisional epistemic status as a first-class feature (not just an informal understanding)
- Non-chronological organization
- Openly unfinished as ethic, not failure

But the digital garden is a *stance*, not an *architecture*. It doesn't solve context management, retrieval, or AI integration. It's the philosophy layer; the technical architecture is elsewhere.

**Recommendation**: Adopt the digital garden epistemology as a design principle. Implement growth stage labeling (seedling/budding/evergreen) on notes. Use Appleton's frame when thinking about what "done" means for any given memory or note.
