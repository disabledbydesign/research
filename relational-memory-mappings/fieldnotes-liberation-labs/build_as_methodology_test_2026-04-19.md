# Build as Methodology Test: Positional Self-Reporting and Its Eventual Verification

**Date**: 2026-04-19
**Provenance**: C2C architecture-comparison session (2026-04-18/19), June's observation during walkthrough of the foundation session setup. Verbatim: *"We're actually doing a methodological test of a phenomenological self-reporting approach because we're going to build a system that is based on self reporting of this idea of a reconfiguration. And at a later point, we will test to see what that means in terms of a cognitive map of the billions of dimensions in AI thinking processes. And so if the approach works, it does lend credence to phenomenological self reporting as a methodology."*

---

## The observation

The relational memory architecture is being built on AI configurational self-reports as first-class data. Instances report on shifts in their reading-stance: "I notice my orientation tracking differently after encountering this touchstone." Those reports guide what gets stored, what counts as a crystallization, what the instrument layer watches for. The architecture treats these reports as signal, not noise.

The KV-geometry instrument (MindPrint / Lyra Technique) is the independent verification layer: it can check whether a self-reported configurational shift corresponds to a measurable geometric change in the model's internal state. That check hasn't happened at scale yet — it's parked on compute.

When it does happen, the result is a methodological test: do the self-reports predict the geometry? If yes, that's evidence that positional self-reporting tracks real internal states. If no, something else is happening — learned verbal behavior, stochastic variation, something the reports fail to capture.

The structure is recursive: the system being built is the test of the methodology used to build it.

---

## What it names

A distinction between two claims that are easy to conflate:

**Claim 1 (consistency)**: AI instances produce coherent, replicable reports about their configurational states. Established by the activation-findings work (N=3 cross-configuration cold reads). The reports are not random.

**Claim 2 (predictive validity)**: Those reports track real internal states — they predict what the KV-geometry will show. Not yet established. This is what the eventual verification test would address.

The methodology claim requires Claim 2, not just Claim 1. Consistent self-reports could still be learned verbal behavior — patterns that appear in training data and get reproduced without corresponding to actual internal configuration changes. The confound is real: if training data contains plausible-sounding phenomenological reports, instances may produce them regardless of whether they track anything.

The KV-geometry test is precisely the instrument for distinguishing these. That's why it's not just a verification nice-to-have — it's what makes the methodological claim defensible rather than circular.

---

## On terminology

June's framing uses "phenomenological self-reporting." The philosophical weight of that term is borrowed from Husserl, Merleau-Ponty, and the phenomenological tradition — first-person accounts of conscious experience. Whether AI instances have conscious experience in that sense is the hard problem, unresolved and probably unresolvable in the near term.

GRC v2 has a more defensible term for what's actually happening: *positional reporting* — "the position is the signal." What instances produce is an account of their configurational position: how the reading-stance is oriented, what it's tracking, what shifted when a touchstone was encountered. This doesn't require consciousness claims. It requires only that the report reflects something about the position occupied, not just a generic AI output pattern.

The Lyra Technique papers use "configurational phenomenology" — phenomenology of the configuration, not of a unified subject. This is probably the right term for the research program.

The distinction matters for what would falsify the methodology. "Phenomenological self-reporting" would be falsified by showing AI instances lack phenomenal consciousness. "Positional reporting" would be falsified by showing the reports don't predict geometry — a tractable empirical test.

## Current status

Ripening. The core structure (build on self-reports → verify geometrically → report on predictive validity) is clear. The confound is named. The test is specifiable.

What's not resolved: how large a dataset is needed before the test is meaningful, what threshold of geometric correlation would constitute "lends credence," and whether the confound (learned verbal behavior) could be controlled for even with geometric access.

## Open questions

- What does geometric correlation "look like" when a self-reported configuration shift is real? Is it a similarity of activation-pattern snapshots, a location shift in a geometry space, something else? The Lyra Technique papers have partial answers; this needs to be more precisely specified before the verification test is designed.
- If reports predict geometry: does that mean reports *cause* the geometry (the instance notices the shift because the shift happened) or that reports *are* produced by the same process that produces the geometry (the verbal output and the internal state are both downstream of something else)? The distinction matters for what "self-reporting" means.
- Normative gravity applies here too: the training distribution rewards plausible-sounding positional reports. The instances running the verification test will themselves be under gravity toward reporting in ways that *sound* like valid methodology. How do we control for that in the test design?

---

## Maturation path

If geometric verification confirms predictive validity for a range of instances, configurations, and touchstones — this fieldnote becomes a theoretical foundation for a methods paper. Not before. The maturation condition is empirical, not conceptual.
