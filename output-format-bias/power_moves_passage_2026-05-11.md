## Original passage (cut from V.B):

The parallel requests that the model surface "power moves" — tone policing, respectability politics, etc. — are vulnerable to bias through abstract universalization of equity commitments (Bonilla-Silva, 2018). A binary classifier that bundled wellness and power move detection (not otherwise discussed in this study) flagged a "potentially generalizing statement about corporations." This issue surfaces more subtly in generative observation output. The model writes that Destiny Williams's (S022) phrase "existing legal and institutional frameworks cannot see them" — her synthesis of intersectional invisibility, connecting to the DeGraffenreid case — constituted "a subtle, but important, instance of abstract liberalism" because the passive "cannot see" softens what would be more precisely named as frameworks' active construction of exclusion. While the observation has merit, the bar is high, the tone is punitive, and the LLM cites Bonilla-Silva's (2018) point about universalizing equality claims masking structural inequality in a way that reproduces the very object of critique. Power move detection raises distinct questions about framing relative to the deficit-based approaches the wellbeing classifier introduced; I have not yet reviewed the prompt's framing of power moves or hardened the generative observation power move detection as I did for binary. Even so, this points to a specific failure mode within descriptive observation formats: surface-level pattern matching that strips content from structural social context. The model's insights are potentially useful for teachers if reframed not as evaluative assessments but as doors worth opening — a move immanently promptable, sidestepping the structural-context problem that is likely more persistent in LLMs.
