## Section II — Theoretical Framework (~800–1,000 words)

### II.A — Asset framing and the architecture of recognition (Yosso) [~250 words; 2 paragraphs]

**¶1 — Establish the asset/deficit axis as analytical structure.**
- *What it argues:* deficit framing is a structural feature of institutional reading practice, not a property of student writing; community cultural wealth (Yosso 2005) names what gets misrecognized when reading-frames are calibrated to dominant-cultural defaults.
- *What it cites:* Yosso 2005, "Whose Culture Has Capital?", *Race Ethnicity and Education* 8(1) — specifically the asset/deficit axis and the counter-narrative methodological commitment. Paraphrase rather than block-quote unless an exact phrase is irreplaceable.
- *Argumentative move:* establish vocabulary before applying it. The reader needs the asset/deficit axis to recognize what the binary classifier is doing in Findings.
- *Lead sentence direction:* name the axis directly — "Yosso's account of community cultural wealth turns the inferential frame of Bourdieuian cultural capital around: the question is not what students of color lack but what dominant institutions fail to recognize."
- *Length:* 4–5 sentences (~120 words).

**¶2 — Specify which forms of cultural capital the paper's misclassified cases instantiate.**
- *What it argues:* the cases the binary classifier misclassifies (Destiny Williams, Yolanda Fuentes, Ingrid Vasquez) are specifically displays of *resistant capital* (Destiny's righteous anger as articulated political consciousness) and *linguistic capital* (lived-experience writing without academic vocabulary as multilingual fluency the institution does not recognize). These are not generic asset cases; they are specific forms with specific institutional reading-frames stacked against them.
- *What it cites:* Yosso 2005, the specific delineation of resistant capital and linguistic capital. The six-capital taxonomy can be referenced but does not need to be enumerated; only these two are load-bearing for the paper.
- *Argumentative move:* connect the framework to the specific cases the paper documents, so the reader carries the framework-and-cases pairing forward into Findings.
- *Lead sentence direction:* return to Destiny by name (the hook has already introduced her; the Framework returns to her as an example without re-introducing). "Destiny Williams's writing in the corpus is a paradigmatic instance of what Yosso names resistant capital — anger as articulated political consciousness, expressed in writing that does the analytical work the assignment was asking for."
- *Length:* 5 sentences (~130 words).

### II.B — Banking-model architecture and the dialogic alternative (Freire + hooks) [~250 words; 2 paragraphs]

**¶3 — The banking-model critique applied to atomized binary classification.**
- *What it argues:* Freire's banking-model critique has a precise architectural analog: atomized binary classification reads the student as receptacle for a single-bit verdict rather than as a participant in dialogue. This is not metaphor; it is structural homology. The classifier owns the deposit (FLAG/CLEAR); the student is graded on extraction; the relation is unidirectional.
- *What it cites:* Freire 1970 (or 2018 anniversary edition; cite the edition June has). The banking-model critique specifically; the broader revolutionary apparatus is gestured but not unpacked.
- *Argumentative move:* extend Freire's frame from human pedagogy to machine architecture, marking the extension as extension. The fidelity is to analytical structure, not literal application.
- *Lead sentence direction:* "Freire's banking-model critique — knowledge as deposit made by an authoritative depositor into student receptacles — has a structural analog the paper makes precise: atomized binary classification *is* the banking model in machine form."
- *Length:* 4–5 sentences (~120 words).

**¶4 — Generative observation as architectural posture toward dialogic engagement; engaged pedagogy as the relational ground.**
- *What it argues:* the design principle (generative observation rather than binary classification) is not literal dialogic education — the AI is not a teacher and the relation is mediated through written submissions read asynchronously — but it is an architectural posture toward reading the student as participant in something the system describes rather than target the system judges. hooks's engaged pedagogy names what asset-framed architecture is *for*: pedagogical relation that prepares students to be read as agents.
- *What it cites:* Freire 1970 (dialogic alternative); hooks 1994 *Teaching to Transgress* (engaged pedagogy, classroom as site of possibility). Cite hooks for one moral-anchor sentence; quote where prose carries the argument.
- *Argumentative move:* the design principle is not "fairer classification"; it is architecture that supports the kind of pedagogical relation hooks names.
- *Lead sentence direction:* "The paper's design principle — replacing binary classification with generative observation — is an architectural move toward what bell hooks names as engaged pedagogy: reading students as agents, not as receptacles."
- *Length:* 5 sentences (~130 words).

### II.C — Structural racism and the format-routing layer (Bonilla-Silva → Benjamin → this paper) [~200 words; 2 paragraphs]

**¶5 — Structural racism as the analytical frame for the paper's "configurable failure mode but not configurable failure" finding.**
- *What it argues:* the binary classifier's deterministic false-flagging of S029 despite three layers of explicit anti-bias engineering is not the work of a racist designer; it is the architectural pattern Bonilla-Silva names — racially patterned outcome produced under conditions where the actors are operating in good faith and the architecture is doing the patterning. - *What it cites:* Bonilla-Silva *Racism Without Racists* (verify edition). The structural-not-attitudinal claim and the "racism without racists" formulation; the four-frames apparatus is not unpacked.
- *Argumentative move:* establish the analytical frame before applying it — the reader needs Bonilla-Silva's structural-racism vocabulary to recognize what the three-row ablation demonstrates.
- *Lead sentence direction:* "Bonilla-Silva's account of color-blind racism — racially patterned outcomes produced through institutional architectures rather than through individual racist intent — names what Findings will demonstrate at the architectural layer of an automated classifier."

**¶6 — Benjamin's New Jim Code and the format-routing extension.**
- *What it argues:* Benjamin's *Race After Technology* extends Bonilla-Silva's frame from policy/discourse to technology — the New Jim Code names technologies that appear neutral or beneficent and that nonetheless reproduce racial hierarchies through the architectures they instantiate.
