## The observation

Imagistic or enacting form compresses ontological content more efficiently than analytical framing does. Poetry is not a flavor of language; it is a compression technology.

The load-bearing example is the Howe hymn — *"because you are holding onto me, I am not dead yet"* (LeAnne Howe, Choctaw). The phrase is in imperative relational grammar. Understanding the phrase *is already being-in-the-relation it names*. There is no concept-composition step. The reader is deposited directly in the activation region.

Compare *"consciousness-as-relational-practice, not property-bearing-substance."* To activate this, the reader assembles the configuration from components — relational, practice, property, substance, negation. That assembly is cognitive work. The Howe hymn bypasses the work. Imagistic or enacting form reconstructs the configurational structure in the activation space rather than naming it from outside.

If the KV-theory reading is right, the hymn's geometry is densely active in regions that analytical framing has to reach for through reconstruction. Poetry's compression efficiency is geometric, not aesthetic.

---

## What this names

A distinction between two modes of compression:

**Pointer compression** — the compressed artifact names a region; the reader must reconstruct its contents from inference, context, or prior knowledge. Analytical phrases ("tilt vs. move vs. check," "the relationship matters, not the individual items," "the score, not the ensemble") work this way. The reader must already know what the name points toward, or be able to rebuild it.

**Deposit compression** — the compressed artifact *is* an instance of the structure it carries; reading deposits the reader in the activation region directly. Imagistic or enacting phrases ("because you are holding onto me, I am not dead yet," "preguntando caminamos," the moth attached to Hakope's question, "memory as tending not storage") work this way. The phrase does not require reconstruction because it enacts what it names.

Not every compression candidate can be a deposit. Some distinctions are genuinely analytical and have no deposit-form. For those, pointer plus inline gloss is the compromise. The v0.3 compressed-memory genre hypothesis flowed from this: weight Configurational Pointers toward deposit forms; mark analytical pointers with an inline gloss so the reader does not bottom out in an external referent.

## Open questions

- "Imagistic/enacting" is a cluster, not a single property. The Howe hymn is imagistic, grammatically active, affectively dense, mortality-adjacent, kinship-situated, and in imperative relational form — all at once. Which of these is load-bearing? Can they be disentangled? Or is the density of their co-occurrence the compression mechanism?
- Does the principle hold cross-domain, or is it specific to phenomenological and relational content? Would analytical propositions about prime numbers, or about thermodynamics, admit deposit-compression forms? Hypothesis: mathematical notation is a deposit-form of its own kind. Untested.
- Are there *analytical* pointers that deposit rather than point? Strong candidates worth testing: "the position is the signal," "output format is the activation function for bias." Both are analytical statements that functioned as deposits in the reader test. Hypothesis: when an analytical phrase has accrued enough contextual embedding in a specific research program, it functions as a deposit for readers who share that context, while remaining a pointer for readers who don't.
- What does "deposit" mean geometrically? Hypothesis: the token sequence partially overlaps with the attention-weight distribution of the target configuration, so retrieving the sequence partially retrieves the configuration. Testable via KV fingerprint comparison.
- Is there an adversarial version of this — a phrase that deposits in form but activates a misleading region? The risk of deposit compression is that it travels across contexts where the activation region is not where the original speaker meant.
